# The blue wave lasted one trading day

Democrats won the 2026 midterms decisively. They took at least 54 Senate seats, with two Republican-held seats still uncounted, and roughly 250 House seats. They flipped five governorships and swept Arizona, which returned Attorney General Kris Mayes, the official prosecuting Kalshi. Markets traded the result for a single session. On Wednesday Nov 4 the Dow jumped **3.03%** in a textbook "blue trade": Centene rose 12.6% and HCA 10.3%, and solar beat oil producers by about 13 points, while defense primes, private-prison operators and AI "neocloud" stocks fell. On Thursday an AI and chip crash erased the rally: the S&P 500 fell **4.4%**, the Nasdaq 5.5% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index 12.2%. On Friday a strong jobs report pushed the odds of a December Fed hike to about **77–80%**, and the S&P 500 finished **down 2.68%** for its worst week since April 2025. The owner's "blue wave" framing is half right. Defense, private prisons, Musk- and Trump-linked names and data-center builders did sour on the result. But banks rallied, energy fell mainly because oil dropped on hopes that the Iran war would end, and crypto rose on the news and then fell with tech. The week's broad losses came from AI, oil and the Fed, not the vote. Democrats lack veto-proof and filibuster-proof majorities, so they will change policy mainly through funding and debt-limit deadlines, oversight and Senate confirmations. Tariffs largely stay, the CLARITY crypto bill dies on Jan 3, 2027, and regulators keep writing crypto rules. For Kalshi in Arizona the courts decide, not Congress. The Ninth Circuit's ruling against Kalshi's sports contracts became final at the circuit level on about Nov 4, Arizona's motion to lift the injunction that protects Kalshi is pending, and Kalshi was still serving Arizona residents as of Nov 5. For Dealbot, the post-election political markets (impeachment, cabinet exits, approval ratings, uncalled races) are news-driven books, the kind that already cost its resting-order desk more than the rewards it earned. The best fits are near-dated contracts with one settlement source and a known calendar of catalysts, such as the Dec 12 shutdown and Dec 9 Fed contracts, quoted only away from those catalysts. Polymarket prices are worth reading as an early warning, not as an arbitrage. Every figure in this report comes from search-result summaries rather than full pages, because the session's network settings blocked the source sites; the report flags figures that rest on one source or do not reconcile, and the last section lists them.

## The "blue wave" framing fits defense and data centers, not banks, oil or the index

The owner's framing holds that a blue wave soured sentiment on sectors that benefited from Republican control. The evidence supports a narrower claim. On one day, the result repriced the sectors whose profits depend on federal money or federal permission in 2027–28. It did not sour "Republican trades" across the board, and it does not explain the week's losses.

The framing fits five groups. **Defense** sold off on doubt that the White House's $1.5 trillion FY2027 request survives a Democratic Congress; Lockheed had been up 32.11% for the year before the vote ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/11/62858554/lockheed-martin-rtx-northrop-stocks-fall-after-democratic-midterm-sweep-raises-questions-over-trumps-defense-spending-plan)). **Private prisons** fell after GEO had rallied 74% this year ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/ai-data-center-stocks-dip-113427491.html)). **Musk- and Trump-linked names**, including the newly listed SpaceX and Tesla, came under pressure ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/politics/26/11/62862271/midterm-blue-sweep-trump-trade-spacex-tesla-defense-stocks)). **Data-center builders** fell because the election made them a voting issue: 56% of voters in an NBC exit poll called AI and data centers a "crucial or major factor," and all but one of 41 local data-center ballot measures in 10 states were on track to pass ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/11/04/midterm-elections-results-ai-industry-data-centers/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91635796/7-ways-the-midterms-may-affect-the-ai-boom)). **Crypto stocks** fell 10–12% on Nov 5, amid talk that market-structure legislation would stall.

Four facts complicate it. First, **financials led the S&P 500** on Nov 4 and European banks had their best day since May, although bank deregulation is a classic Republican trade. IFRE's explanation is that the gains from deregulation are "already in the bank" and hard to reverse without a veto-proof majority ([IFRE](https://www.ifre.com/opinion/2508436/macaskill-on-markets-wall-street-can-bank-its-gains-after-the-us-midterms)). Second, **energy fell because of the election, but through the war rather than through energy policy**. Crude was in its fourth straight daily decline as traders bet Republican losses would reduce escalation risk in the Iran war ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/business/5337828-gold-rallies-dollar-treasury-yields-soften-after-us-midterm-results)), and Fortune also cites weaker expected demand from data centers ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/seismic-blue-wave-sinking-oil-prices-energy-stocks-power-permitting-reform/)). Third, **crypto rose on the result**, tracking stocks higher on Nov 4 ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-11-04/crypto-tracks-stocks-higher-after-democrat-midterm-wins)), and fell the next day with the AI rout. Fourth, **sentiment toward the market as a whole improved on the result**: the Dow had its best day in 19 months, and hyperscalers rallied while neoclouds fell. The souring came on Thursday from chip earnings, deleveraging and AI-capex doubts, of which the election was only one strand.

History says day-one rotations rarely last. In 2006, the closest analog (a Democratic sweep under a Republican president at war), drugmakers and defense stocks fell the day after the vote while the Dow closed at a record. Goldman argued then that defense spending was unlikely to decline ([CNNMoney](https://money.cnn.com/2006/11/08/markets/election_winners_losers/index.htm); [Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2006/11/09/wall-streets-election-winners-and-losers)). Wolfe Research's Myles Walton cites that precedent to call the 2026 defense selloff likely short-lived ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/369d72db-cac0-4c51-87af-d12a23f8e298)). In 2018, when Democrats won only the House, insurers and drugmakers rallied as the threat of ACA repeal faded ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/morning-breakout/health-care-stocks-soar-as-threat-to-health-law-fades-with-democrats-house-victory/)). BlackRock's summary of midterm years since 1990 is that sector leadership "rarely followed a party script" ([BlackRock](https://www.blackrock.com/us/financial-professionals/insights/2026-midterm-elections-and-market-performance)). The index-level base rate is friendlier than this week suggests, but thin. Divided government under a Republican president averaged an 18% gain in the following 12 months and was positive in 13 of 14 cases since 1874 ([Motley Fool Research](https://www.fool.com/research/midterm-election-year-stock-market-history/)). With 14 observations, and a link between party control and returns that Research Affiliates found does not replicate in five other markets ([Research Affiliates](https://www.researchaffiliates.com/publications/articles/614-presidential-politics-and-stock-returns-is-the-relation-real-or-spurious)), that is a weak prior, not a signal.

## Gridlock leaves tariffs standing and moves every fight to a deadline

Without veto-proof or filibuster-proof majorities, a Democratic Congress has four tools: refuse to pass must-pass bills without concessions, investigate, block nominees, and threaten. Capital Economics says the size of the gains "does not change too much for the near-term policy outlook" ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/markets/us-midterms-how-markets-are-reacting-democrat-victory), attribution not verified). The leverage points are dated, and they set the calendar for every sector below.

| Area | What can realistically change | Vehicle and timing | What markets priced on Nov 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tariffs | Selected rollbacks for a few countries or products | Concessions at the 2027 debt limit or FY2028 funding (due Oct 1, 2027); court cases | Relief rally in alcohol importers and homebuilders |
| Crypto | No market-structure law; hearings; blocked nominees | CLARITY dies Jan 3, 2027; SEC and CFTC rulemaking continues | Crypto stocks down 10–12% on Nov 5 |
| Energy | Protect the incentives that remain; restoring repealed credits needs Trump's signature | Must-pass packages | Solar up, oil down (the war channel) |
| Defense | A lower topline than $1.5 trillion; fights over Iran-war money and Hegseth | FY2027 appropriations and NDAA; FY2028 budget | Primes down 3–7% |
| Healthcare | ACA subsidies and delays to Medicaid cuts, via negotiated deals | Lame duck before Dec 11, then 2027 packages | Hospitals and Medicaid insurers up 9–13% |
| Financial regulation | Hearings, pressure on mergers and buybacks, confirmations; no rollback of rules | Senate Banking under Warren from January 2027 | Banks up |
| Prediction markets | Hearings and subpoenas; ban bills unlikely to be signed | Courts decide (next section) | Not applicable |

**Trade and tariffs.** The courts reshaped tariffs before the voters did. On Feb 20, 2026 the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/scotus-rejects-ieepa-tariffs-refund-opportunities-and-uncertainty-emerge)). The administration replied the same day with a 10% Section 122 surcharge ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-trump-responds-with-section-122-global-surcharge/)). The Court of International Trade struck that surcharge down in May for three importer plaintiffs, in a ruling that is on appeal and not final ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs)). The administration now relies on Sections 232 and 301, which need no vote of Congress ([Seoul Economic Daily/KOTRA](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/11/05/tariffs-to-stay-after-us-democratic-sweep-kotra-says)). Jeffries promises to eliminate the Trump tariffs within the first 100 days ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/05/nx-s1-6029331/democrats-have-a-long-to-do-list-when-they-take-over-congress-next-year)), with the first votes due within days of the Jan 3 swearing-in ([InsideTrade](https://insidetrade.com/daily-news/house-democrats-eye-tariffs-priority-first-hundred-hours-2027)). Such bills still need 60 Senate votes and face a veto. Goldman's Alec Phillips and Abhay Duggirala expect Democrats instead to use fiscal deadlines to win selected rollbacks focused on a few countries, such as Canada, or on particular products ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/06/economy/job-market-midterms)). KOTRA expects the tariffs to stay, Bloomberg expects the White House to keep its approach through 2028, and executives told Reuters the win will not ease their tariff costs in the near term ([Bloomberg Professional](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/insights/regional-analysis/us-midterm-elections-five-sectors-to-watch/); [US News/Reuters](https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2026-11-04/us-democratic-win-unlikely-to-ease-firms-cost-tariff-woes-in-near-term-executives-say)). Wednesday's rally in tariff-relief names therefore priced a low-probability outcome. The likelier changes come from bargaining at the deadlines and from court cases, including refunds of IEEPA duties. One gap remains: these notes do not show what replaced the Section 122 surcharge after its 150-day statutory limit, which would have run out around late July 2026.

**Crypto legislation.** The CLARITY Act, the market-structure bill, passed the House 294–134 in July 2025. On Sept 15, 2026, a Senate cloture vote on the motion to proceed failed 49–50, and the bill expires with the 119th Congress on Jan 3, 2027 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/clarity-act-faces-new-test-under-democratic-congress)). No lame-duck vehicle was found. Democrats are discussing a broader replacement that gives the SEC more oversight and adds ethics restrictions aimed at President Trump's crypto interests. That reporting rests on journalists' accounts; no bill has been announced ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/382602/democrats-to-push-broader-crypto-market-structure-bill-after-sweeping-us-midterms-report)). Elizabeth Warren is expected to chair Senate Banking, and Maxine Waters the House Financial Services Committee ([CoinDesk](https://coindesk.com/policy/2026/11/04/tokenization-and-stablecoins-may-weather-democrats-sweep-as-broader-crypto-rules-face-uncertainty)). A bill built around the SEC and limits on the President's own interests is unlikely to get Trump's signature, and TD Cowen's Jaret Seiberg sees little chance of market-structure legislation in the next two years ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/50508), attribution not verified). The agencies will set policy instead. SEC Chair Paul Atkins pledged to act "with or without legislation," and CFTC Chair Mike Selig says his agency is "ready to ship" its crypto rules ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/378408/cftc-sec-double-down-crypto-clarity-act)). Seward & Kissel's Kris Swiatek does not expect the midterms "to materially slow the digital asset agenda" ([CoinDesk](https://coindesk.com/policy/2026/11/04/tokenization-and-stablecoins-may-weather-democrats-sweep-as-broader-crypto-rules-face-uncertainty)). Two risks remain. A Democratic Senate can block new SEC and CFTC nominees from January, and rules the agencies write can be reversed by a later administration. Stablecoin payments and tokenization, which run under existing law, look the least exposed.

**Energy.** The clean-energy gain is a reduction in risk, not new money. Democratic leaders pledged to restore the wind and solar tax credits cut in 2025 ([Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/economy/clean-energy-stocks-midterms)), but that needs Trump's signature. Barclays frames the benefit as the result "potentially reduces the risk of further political interference with renewable energy incentives" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/midterm-election-blue-wave-trump-renewable-energy.html)). One analysis says the rally reflects "policy risk going down, which is not the same as the economics getting better" ([Gaining Green](https://gaininggreen.com/article/blue-trade-clean-energy-ai-infrastructure)). TD Cowen cut its odds of permitting reform passing in the lame duck from 40% to 25%, a modest negative for pipelines, transmission and some renewable developers ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/seismic-blue-wave-sinking-oil-prices-energy-stocks-power-permitting-reform/)). UBS expects Congress to go no further than the states on data-center power, with data centers increasingly paying for grid upgrades, which cuts both ways for utilities ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/new-political-landscape-facing-ai-after-democrats-midterm-gains)). Oil and gas answer to Iran more than to Congress. No post-election coverage of coal, federal leasing or LNG export policy turned up.

**Defense.** The White House wants $1.5 trillion for FY2027, against $901 billion currently approved. Jefferies expects the final number to land closer to the House's proposal of about $1.15 trillion ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/05/defense-stocks-sank-as-democrats-took-congress-and-the-1-5-trillion-budget-looks-doomed/)). The spending level is the main unfinished dispute in the lame-duck NDAA ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/beyond-the-midterms-congress-faces-converging-year-end-deadlines)). Democrats plan Iran-war bills in their first 100 hours, which Trump can veto ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/newsletters/morning-report/6200194-2026-midterms-results-iran-war-donald-trump-democrats/)). Jeffries says "one of the first things" the majority will do is "run Pete Hegseth out of town," and Democrats have already filed articles of impeachment against the defense secretary ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/lawyer-up-dems-immediately-put-trump-notice-amid-republicans-midterm-bloodbath)). Some Senate Democrats float his removal as the price of passing any of Trump's defense programs ([Slate](https://slate.com/news-and-politics/2026/11/democrats-midterms-senate-house-trump-iran-hegseth.html)). The war-powers votes are mostly signals, so the real constraint is appropriations. China deterrence, nuclear modernization, missile defense and shipbuilding look best protected, and Morgan Stanley names shipbuilding, autonomous systems and missile defense as opportunities for stock pickers ([Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/2026-us-midterm-election-results-market-impact)). Extra money for the Iran war looks hardest to pass.

**Healthcare.** Hospitals and Medicaid insurers are pricing a deal that does not exist yet. Investors are betting Democrats restore the enhanced ACA subsidies and delay the Medicaid cuts in the 2025 law known as OBBBA or H.R. 1, "but no law has changed yet" ([Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/hospital-stocks-rise-democratic-congress-medicaid-aca-bets-480884)). Restoring the subsidies needs 60 Senate votes, and Trump has said he may veto an extension ([Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/us-midterms-2026-healthcare-impact/)). Bernstein expects negotiated delays to parts of H.R. 1 and a compromise on the marketplace subsidies ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/post-midterm-healthcare-stock-report/), attribution not verified). T. Rowe Price's Gil Fortgang expects a focus on moderating some Medicaid cuts, and Wolfe Research's Tobin Marcus calls healthcare "the biggest focus" for Democrats ([ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/active-etf-content-hub/health-care-stocks-face-policy-split-midterm/)). The Dec 11 funding deadline makes both issues live in the lame duck ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/04/lame-duck-session-congress-health-policy-aca-subsidies-medicaid-funding/)). Drug pricing is a slower risk: Citi expects the existing most-favored-nation drug-pricing deals to stay intact under louder hearings, and Leerink and RBC say Democrats have bigger priorities ([Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/us-midterms-2026-healthcare-impact/)). The S&P 500 healthcare sector was already up more than 19% for the year at record highs before the vote ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/midterm-election-stock-market.html)). Hospitals and Medicaid managed-care stocks therefore have more room to fall than to rise if talks fail or a veto holds.

**Financial regulation and the Fed.** Banks' deregulation gains are mostly locked in. The easing of capital rules is essentially complete, and Warren is expected to push for oversight, consumer protections, merger blocking and limits on buybacks ([IFRE](https://www.ifre.com/opinion/2508436/macaskill-on-markets-wall-street-can-bank-its-gains-after-the-us-midterms)). Forbes expects her to ask whether capital requirements reflect exposure to private credit and other nonbank lenders. It notes that a committee chair cannot direct individual exams or set capital requirements, and works instead through hearings, subpoenas, confirmations and public pressure ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/mayrarodriguezvalladares/2026/11/04/a-democratic-landslide-could-reshape-us-banking-regulation/)). Annual testimony by big-bank CEOs is likely to return ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/elizabeth-warren-will-take-over-the-senate-banking-committee-next-year-expect-hearings-205713342.html)). For the Fed, Reuters reports that Senate control is a major check on Trump adding allies to the Board. Kevin Warsh was confirmed as chair 54–45 in May 2026, and Warren, a harsh critic, takes over the committee that oversees him ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-11-04/election-stiffens-feds-guardrails-independence-risks-remain)). That arguably lowers the tail risk to Fed independence.

**Detention and AI.** Detention contractors are protected by money already appropriated: $45 billion for ICE detention through Sept 30, 2029, per Trefis, attribution not verified ([Trefis](https://trefis.com/stock/vrt/articles/622737/which-stocks-overreacted-to-the-midterms/2026-11-05)). CoreCivic has sold four detention centers to DHS for $2.2 billion this year and acknowledges its ICE contracts can be terminated ([Washington Sun/NOTUS](https://www.washingtonsun.com/immigration/sell-ice-detention-centers?redirected=notus_org)). For AI, federal preemption of state AI laws, a White House goal, is now harder ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/11/04/midterm-elections-results-ai-industry-data-centers/)). Most lawmakers in both parties reject the Sanders and Ocasio-Cortez proposal for a national moratorium on data centers ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/democrats-data-center-restrictions-midterms.html)). The binding risk to data-center developers is therefore state and local siting and power rules, not federal law.

**The calendar.** H.R. 6500, signed on Sept 2, funds the government through **Dec 11, 2026**, and none of the 12 regular FY2027 bills has been enacted ([ASME](https://www.asme.org/government-relations/policy-impact/congress-prevents-government-shutdown-and-delays-omb-rule)). A clean continuing resolution is not expected to struggle for 60 Senate votes ([HNGN](https://www.hngn.com/articles/273714/20261105/lame-duck-congress-returns-funding-deadline-democrats-waiting.htm), attribution not verified). Still, this Congress has already shut the government down twice in 2026: for 4 days from Jan 31 and for 76 days from Feb 14 to Apr 30 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_federal_government_shutdowns)). The debt was $40.547 trillion against a $41.104 trillion limit on Nov 3 ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/05/wall-street-is-already-bracing-for-a-2027-debt-ceiling-fight/)). Treasury expects to reach the limit between late winter and mid-summer 2027, and Wall Street puts the X-date around the fourth quarter of 2027 ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/09/09/unsustainable-fiscal-path-debt-limit-fight-awaits-congress/)). CNBC calls the debt ceiling the "only shot" Democrats have at concessions ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/democrats-trump-debt-ceiling-fight.html)). FY2028 funding is due Oct 1, 2027. These deadlines, not the election, will decide whether Wednesday's sector moves hold.

## Kalshi's record election night gave way to thinner, news-driven books

The midterms were the largest election yet for US-regulated prediction markets, but election flow stayed a sideshow on Kalshi. Kalshi's company-reported recap cites more than **$2.5 billion** in midterm trading, against $1.3 billion for the whole 2024 cycle; that is notional volume, and it disclosed no trader profit or loss ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/kalshi-midterm-election-trading-volume-2-5-billion/); [KHOU](https://www.khou.com/article/news/politics/kalshi-2026-election-trading/285-1d5d55ec-d9d1-4e95-a53e-426c84fdf9d4)). Election contracts traded **$729 million** on Nov 3 alone, per Paradigm's Matt Huang ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2302611)). Platform-wide volume that day was $4.15 billion, below the $5.30 billion October record. The five largest election series, led by the Texas Senate race, traded about $440 million, while the 15-minute bitcoin series alone traded **$332.6 million**, more than any single election series ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/igaming/kalshi-election-day-volume-hits-4-15b-short-october-record/)). Kalshi's busiest weeks run about $19–21.5 billion, about two-thirds of it combos, which are mostly sports parlays ([Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/kalshi-weekly-trading-volume-record-trades/)). Polymarket US traded more than $234 million in political markets over the 30 days around the vote, about 47% of it on Nov 3–4 ([The Block](https://x.com/TheBlockCo/status/2118436586098467182)). Between midnight and 6 a.m. ET on election night, Kalshi traded $127.4 million across 11 midterm markets against Polymarket's $30.6 million, yet Polymarket called the House first ([Gaming America](https://gamingamerica.com/news/1109871/kalshi-polymarket-midterms-night-volume-house-senate)). That is a single observation, but it suggests the smaller venue can lead.

The markets got the big calls right and missed some upsets. Both venues priced a Democratic House above 90% before the first polls closed. Kalshi claims 95% accuracy on House races and 94% on Senate races, and Polymarket claims 98.4%, all self-reported and measured differently ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/sports-gaming/6199204-prediction-markets-midterm-accuracy/)). Traders missed the Democratic wins in the South Carolina Senate race and the Texas governor's race, and priced Iowa's Senate race near 50-50 ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/11/06/prediction-markets-called-the-midterms-lawmakers-still-call-them-gambling/)). Senate control went from 75% on Kalshi and 75.5% on Polymarket just before the vote ([SportsBettingDime](https://www.sportsbettingdime.com/news/politics/kalshi-polymarket-election-odds-comparison/)) to near 100% by 9 p.m. ET ([Yahoo/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/prediction-markets-face-us-midterms-110151053.html)). The independent evidence is more modest than the company claims. The gap between three markets and two poll-based models on the Senate averaged 7.3 points from Sept 1 and narrowed to 2.9 points on election morning ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/prediction-markets-vs-polls-election-forecasting-industry-faces-midterm-test-100000463.html)), and one scoring found Polymarket about a point better than Kalshi on both chambers ([First Sigma](https://firstsigma.substack.com/p/midterm-elections-forecast-comparison-analysis)).

Kalshi's maker rewards now carry most of the incentive load. The Liquidity Incentive Program funds a separate pool for each market, $1 to $1,000 a day. It snapshots resting orders every second, and an order earns a share for its size and closeness to the best price whether or not it fills. Rewards round down to the cent, and a program total under $1 is not paid ([Kalshi Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)). Kalshi's CFTC filings define the score as Discount Factor^ticks × size within a depth window set by a Target Size of 100–20,000 contracts, normalized on each side ([CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09152625924.pdf)). Under a Sept 28 filing, the separate Volume Incentive Program ends no earlier than Oct 13, 2026. Coverage tied that move to scrutiny of about $5 billion in near-identical ether trades, though the filing gives no reason, and Kalshi rejected the wash-trading claims ([Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/kalshi-end-volume-incentive-program-october-13); [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/kalshi-volume-rewards-cftc-scrutinizes-prediction-markets/)). The Block's headline that Kalshi was ending its "liquidity incentive program" conflicts with Kalshi's help center and most other coverage ([The Block](https://theblock.co/news/business/2026-09-30-kalshi-ends-trader-incentive-program-417247)). Kalshi's spending appears to lean toward sports and combos, which have their own programs ([Kalshi Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/17184676-sports-prop-combo-market-component-legs-liquidity-incentive-program)). Which post-election political markets carry programs, and how large, could not be determined; that has to come from Kalshi's Rewards page or Trade API.

Settlement rules diverge across venues exactly where races are close. Kalshi's midterm contracts resolve once 4 of 8 designated media outlets have called a race and none contradicts, and payouts can wait on recounts or legal challenges ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/politics/midterm-election-prediction-markets-payout-rules-2026)). Polymarket US sources outcomes from state electoral authorities and the Senate itself ([Polymarket US](https://polymarket.us/event/usse-midterms-2026-11-03)). Formal disputes are rare: 0.31% of 529,408 Polymarket markets were challenged ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29578v1)). Still, a trader argued in September that the Alaska Senate market's rules did not match its title ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/24/politics/midterm-elections-prediction-markets-kalshi-polymarket)), and Polymarket has investigated dozens of midterm bets, referring at least two cases to the CFTC ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/27/nx-s1-6012304/polymarket-election-betting-surveillance)). No formal settlement dispute on a November 2026 contract had surfaced by Nov 7.

## Courts, not Congress, will decide whether Arizona keeps Kalshi

Congress gains a spotlight on prediction markets, not a veto-proof ban. From January, Democrats get hearing and subpoena power over the CFTC and Chair Selig, and over Donald Trump Jr.'s roles as a paid strategic adviser to Kalshi and a member of Polymarket's advisory board ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/democrats-prediction-markets-cftc-midterms.html)). The bipartisan bills to ban sports and casino-style contracts are S. 4160, the Prediction Markets Are Gambling Act from Sens. Schiff, Curtis and Cortez Masto, and its House companion H.R. 9856 from Reps. Horsford and Amodei. Both have only been referred to the agriculture committees ([Congress.gov S. 4160](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4160/text); [Congress.gov H.R. 9856](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9856/text); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/prediction-markets-could-face-bipartisan-reckoning-after-midterms/)). Any ban would face a president whose administration defends the industry. Narrower bills on insider trading by senior federal officials, such as the PREDICT Act, have better odds ([Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/congress-predictably-turns-its-attention-to-prediction-markets), attribution not verified). Warren plans oversight of prediction markets from the Banking chair ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/featured/crypto-industry-braces-tougher-rules-after-democrats-midterms/)).

The CFTC is escalating against the states. On Nov 5 it gave designated contract markets, and the brokers that route orders to them, **30 days to lift state-based geoblocks or explain why they cannot**. The interpretation is not a court ruling and undoes no state order, and iGaming Today reads the "explain" option as the operative part ([SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/11/06/cftc-prediction-markets-unblock-states/); [iGaming Today](https://www.igamingtoday.com/cftc-orders-prediction-markets-to-lift-state-geo-blocks-within-30-days/)). The CFTC sued Utah in late October and has also sued Rhode Island, and Legal Sports Report counts 10 states in litigation ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/285917/what-comes-next-for-prediction-markets-after-democrats-take-over/)).

The courts are moving against Kalshi. On Aug 28 a Ninth Circuit panel held in Kalshi's Nevada case that sports event contracts are not swaps and are not shielded from state gambling law. It carved out election contracts and sent the question of Nevada's authority over them back to the district court ([Arizona Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/08/28/9th-circuit-sides-with-states-in-kalshi-gambling-fight-potentially-reviving-arizonas-prosecution/)). On about Nov 4 the court unanimously denied panel rehearing and rehearing en banc, with "no judge" requesting a vote. That gives Kalshi until about **Feb 2, 2027** to petition the Supreme Court ([CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/u-s-appeals-court-denies-kalshis-petition-for-rehearing-in-nevada-case/); [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/ninth-circuit-rejects-kalshi-rehearing/)). The ruling splits with the Third Circuit's Apr 6 decision for Kalshi in New Jersey. Robinhood has already petitioned the Supreme Court, and Crypto.com reportedly has too ([Sports Betting Dime](https://www.sportsbettingdime.com/news/industry/kalshi-files-for-ninth-circuit-en-banc-hearing-while-robinhood-requests-cert-petition-with-scotus/); [NextPredict](https://nextpredict.io/market-news/legal/ninth-circuit-denies-kalshi-rehearing/)). Kalshi has also lost injunction bids in New York and Connecticut and at the Second Circuit ([MLQ.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/kalshi-loses-injunction-bids-in-new-york-and-connecticut-as-state-gambling-fight-widens/)). On Nov 6 it was blocked from California tribal lands ([SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/11/06/kalshi-california-tribes-injunction)).

Arizona's case is one court order away from reviving. Mayes filed a 20-count criminal case on Mar 17, 2026, the first state criminal case against a prediction market ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/17/nx-s1-5751165/kalshi-criminal-charges-arizona)). It alleges an unlicensed wagering business and illegal betting on Arizona elections, and four counts reportedly concern the 2026 governor and secretary of state races and the 2028 presidential race ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-shields-kalshi-election-wagering-in-arizona/), attribution of the four-count detail not verified). The CFTC and DOJ sued Arizona on Apr 2 and won a restraining order on Apr 10 ([azfamily](https://www.azfamily.com/2026/04/10/federal-judge-halts-arizona-criminal-case-against-kalshi/)). On May 5, Judge Michael Liburdi converted it into an injunction that also covers Arizona's election-betting law. The Arizona Mirror calls that block permanent, while a litigation tracker calls it preliminary ([Arizona Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/05/05/arizonas-criminal-case-against-kalshi-permanently-blocked-by-federal-judge/); [Litigation Tracker](https://mickbransfield.com/2025/08/11/summary-of-legal-actions-involving-kalshis-sports-event-contracts/)). Arizona appealed on Jul 6, and on Sept 14 it asked the Ninth Circuit to vacate the injunction in light of the Nevada ruling ([Prediction News](https://predictionnews.com/story/arizona-asks-ninth-circuit-to-vacate-ruling-against-sports-event-contracts-citin)). The CFTC conceded that Liburdi's reasoning "cannot stand" but asked the court to wait for the Nevada rehearing decision ([Arizona Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/09/25/after-9th-circuit-ruling-mayes-pushes-to-revive-her-gambling-case-against-kalshi/)). That decision has now come, and commentator Daniel Wallach says it clears the way for the court to act ([NextPredict](https://nextpredict.io/market-news/legal/ninth-circuit-denies-kalshi-rehearing/)). No ruling had surfaced by Nov 7. Meanwhile CBS Sports listed Arizona as live for Kalshi's sports contracts as of Nov 5 ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/prediction/news/prediction-market-legal-states/)), and Fox Sports calls the state "Contested" but available ([Fox Sports](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/betting/prediction-markets-legal-states)). Sources conflict on whether Polymarket US serves Arizona. One guide says Polymarket US told it in writing on Oct 23 that it serves every state except Nevada, while an August check listed Arizona as blocked ([PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/learn/is-polymarket-legal-in-arizona); [Sharp Football Analysis](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/prediction-markets/polymarket/)).

The likeliest next step follows from those filings. With the Nevada ruling final at the circuit level and the CFTC conceding the district court's reasoning, the Ninth Circuit will probably vacate or narrow the Arizona injunction for sports contracts, possibly within weeks. The re-elected Mayes could then revive the sports counts. Kalshi would be caught between state prosecution, which pushes it to geofence Arizona sports, and the CFTC's new interpretation, which discourages geoblocks; a request for a stay pending a Supreme Court petition is the obvious response. The election-betting counts stand on weaker ground because the Nevada panel carved election contracts out, so political contracts are the least resolved category, not a safe one. For an Arizona resident running a paper-only bot that reads public data, the legal exposure is small. A move to live trading would depend on these rulings, and sports contracts would be the first to go.

## Dealbot should quote the calendar and step aside from the headlines

Dealbot enters this environment knowing two things. Forecasting Kalshi's 15-minute crypto contracts has no edge over Kalshi's own price. Its resting-order desk, which collects liquidity rewards, roughly breaks even on its recording so far, because one news-driven political market cost more than the rewards paid. The next paper round is that desk, strategy E run with risk rules (`lab/strategies/e_desk.py`), starting 2026-11-12T05:00Z after desk v2's week. The loss came from a presidential-approval market that closed on Nov 6. It went to 98¢ against E's position in its last hours and cost about **$109** (the docstring of `lab/strategies/e_desk.py`). The desk's rules already respond to that loss: no new risk in the last 3 hours before a close, a $40 loss stop per market, a $250 cap per market and a $5,000 capital cap. A news-pause variant, which stops adding for 15 minutes after a 5¢ move within 2 minutes, paused 64 times over E's first 81 hours and cost $18 against the same rules without it. The election matters to Dealbot because it multiplied exactly that kind of book. Kalshi's impeachment contract rose 6.6 points between Election Day and Nov 4 ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/trump-impeachment-odds-climb-to-76-after-democratic-midterm-victories)), Vance's nomination odds fell 7 points by the next morning ([Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/knives-out-for-jd-vance-as-his-2028-chances-tank-after-midterms)), and Hegseth's exit odds reached 57% ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/hegseth-exit-impeachment-odds-jump-after-democratic-election-victories)). So the useful question is not which political view to hold, but which books are safe to rest orders in.

**The desk's real exposure is adverse selection in books whose settlement news arrives continuously.** The approval brackets resolve on the RealClearPolling average at 5 p.m. ET on a fixed date ([Lines.com](https://www.lines.com/prediction-markets/politics/trump/trump-approval-rating-on-november-6)). The value of a resting order there changes every time a poll enters the average, and polls cluster after an election. Impeachment-by-date, cabinet-exit and nomination contracts behave the same way, except that the news arrives at random times rather than near the close. The rewards cannot pay for that. A program pays at most $1,000 a day per market, split pro rata, and a program total under $1 is not paid at all ([Kalshi Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)). One picked-off quote at 98¢ wipes out weeks of reward-seconds. The scheduled-catalyst markets are the opposite case. The Dec 12 shutdown contract settles on one public page at 10 a.m. ET ([Kalshi](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxgovtshutdown/will-the-government-shut-down/kxgovtshutdown-26dec12)), and the Fed contract moves mostly on releases with known dates ([PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/tools/fed-rate-tracker/december-2026)). A desk can rest in them most of the time and step aside around a short list of timestamps. Because the E desk's rules are fixed before its start, the blackout belongs in a variant registered before Nov 12 and reported beside the main desk, as the news pause is. Changing the main desk mid-week would break the one-configuration test that the team's rules require.

**Read Kalshi's incentive programs before choosing markets.** These notes could not establish which post-election political or economic markets carry liquidity programs, or how large the pools are. Kalshi publishes the program definitions (market, pool, Target Size, Discount Factor and dates) through its Trade API ([Kalshi Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)). With pro-rata pools and a $1 floor, a small desk earns more by concentrating on a few low-Target-Size programs, where its quotes are a meaningful share of qualifying depth, than by spreading thinly. Near 50¢, resting a full Target Size of 100–500 contracts takes only $50–$250 of collateral per side. Before quoting the Fed series, read each series' fee type. Sources conflict on maker fees ([QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained)), and `lab/strategies/e_incentive.py` records that every series E has quoted so far charges makers nothing, which may not hold for economic contracts.

**Cross-venue gaps are a signal, not a strategy.** The documented gaps were small. Chamber control differed by 0.5–1.5 points, the Nevada governor's race by about 3, and post-election contracts by 1–3 ([SportsBettingDime](https://www.sportsbettingdime.com/news/politics/kalshi-polymarket-election-odds-comparison/); [PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/tools/shutdown-tracker)). A six-month audit found a **median real gap of 0.8¢**, found that every "arbitrage" above 8¢ was a stale quote, and put the profitable threshold near 50¢ at roughly 3–3.5¢ after fees ([PredictMarketCap](https://predictmarketcap.com/analysis/prediction-market-arbitrage-study)). Kalshi's taker fee is 0.07 × P × (1 − P) per contract ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)), and Polymarket US charges about 6.95% × P × (1 − P) ([PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/polymarket-us)). On those formulas, buying Kalshi's House YES at 93¢ and Polymarket's NO at 5.5¢ nets about 0.7¢ per $1 pair before spreads, with capital locked until settlement. The same trade in the Senate market (75¢ against 24.5¢) loses about 2¢. The only structurally viable cross-venue trade for a small bot rests orders on both venues and hedges on a fill. That needs a Polymarket US account, and whether Polymarket US serves Arizona residents is disputed. What Dealbot can use cheaply is the timing. If Polymarket US leads on event nights, as it did for the House call, a Polymarket move on the same event is an early warning to pull Kalshi quotes. That is testable on paper from public prices, as a variant of the news pause keyed to the other venue.

**Leave settlement-tail markets alone, and keep views out of the desk.** The Mississippi and Alaska Senate contracts trade at 94–98%, with recount, ranked-choice and court risk. They pay little for resting, a bad settlement costs the whole stake, and a delayed payout ties up part of the $5,000 cap. Kalshi's rule of 4 of 8 media calls and Polymarket's rule of official results can also diverge in exactly these races ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/politics/midterm-election-prediction-markets-payout-rules-2026)). The shutdown contract offers a tempting view. It sits at 31–36% while commentary expects a clean stopgap to pass ([HNGN](https://www.hngn.com/articles/273714/20261105/lame-duck-congress-returns-funding-deadline-democrats-waiting.htm)), but this Congress already shut the government for 76 days this year. Dealbot's own evidence says Kalshi's price beats its models. If the owner wants a political view, it belongs in a separate paper forecast scored by log loss against the market, as desk v2's forecast was, not in the quoting desk.

**The election changed nothing about the crypto finding.** Election night did not drain Kalshi's crypto books: the 15-minute bitcoin series out-traded every election series on Nov 3 ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/igaming/kalshi-election-day-volume-hits-4-15b-short-october-record/)). Before the election, that book was usually one tick wide, at about 0.6–1.1¢, and makers reprice within seconds off the settlement index ([PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/kalshi-bitcoin-15-minute-last-60-seconds); [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-15-minute-markets)). Bitcoin traded that week as high-beta tech, and its hazard windows for any crypto programs the desk quotes are the same macro dates as for the Fed contract, plus US equity hours on shock days like Nov 5.

**Weight paper results by what an Arizona resident could keep.** Kalshi's incentive spending appears to lean toward sports and combo markets. Those are the contracts the Ninth Circuit ruling exposes in Arizona, and Kalshi may have to geofence them for Arizona users if the injunction falls. Election and political contracts are carved out of that ruling but appear in Mayes' charges. Splitting the E desk's rewards and markouts by category costs nothing on paper. It would show how much of the simulated income depends on the category most likely to vanish for this owner.
