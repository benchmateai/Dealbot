# Leaving Kalshi buys diversification, not bigger edges

No venue that is legal for an Arizona resident offers an automated strategy clearly better than Kalshi's two leaders: capturing Kalshi's liquidity incentives (**35%** confidence of a real edge after fees) and resting bids against long shots (**30%**). The strongest newcomers are slow risk premia reached through a retail broker: merger arbitrage scores **35%** to beat cash after costs and 60% to make any money at all, while closed-end fund discount reversion and micro-futures trend following score **30%** each. All three ignore latency, run on free or cheap data and stand on settled legal ground, but they are paid for carrying crash risk, and no paper week can confirm them. Next come price gaps between Kalshi and a second prediction market, chiefly ForecastEx reached through Interactive Brokers (**25%**, a figure a month of recorded quotes could push above 40% or below 10%). Almost everywhere else, holding cash wins: always-on crypto carry nets about **1–3.7% a year against T-bills near 3.75–4%**, crypto spot arbitrage and rebate farming are fee-blocked at our size, Polymarket US maker rebates start at $10 million of monthly volume, and sportsbook arbitrage typically loses money after 2026's cap on gambling-loss deductions. The most dependable money in Arizona, a one-time sportsbook sign-up sweep worth roughly **$1.5k–$3k before tax** (85%), cannot be run by a bot: the books ban automation and geofence every bet, and the sweep can lose money after tax for anyone who does not itemize. When the paper week ends on November 10, the lab should add a cross-venue gap recorder (Kalshi against ForecastEx, then Polymarket US) and a merger-arbitrage paper book checked against a historical deal backtest, plus, optionally, a no-trade logger of US crypto funding and basis that switches carry on only above about 6% a year. One Interactive Brokers account with a no-withdrawal user reaches ForecastEx, Kalshi, CME micros and US stocks, which makes it the natural second platform.

## A real edge must beat about $400 a year per $10,000 in T-bills

Holding cash sets the bar. The Fed's target range sits at 3.75–4.00% and the two-year Treasury near 3.8% ([Panaprium](https://www.panaprium.com/blogs/i/crypto-basis-trading-evaluate-platforms-risk); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/08/10/a-rare-cme-shift-hedge-funds-abandon-structural-shorts-to-bet-on-a-bitcoin-rally)), so **$10,000 in T-bills earns roughly $375–$400 a year** for no work and almost no risk. A market-neutral strategy that nets 2% after fees therefore loses about $200 a year against doing nothing. Strategies that carry market risk face a higher bar: a put-writing or trend program has to beat a passive fund with the same exposure.

This report scores each strategy on that test. The score is the probability that the strategy beats cash, or its passive equivalent, after every fee, over 6–12 months, or over 2–3 years for the slow risk premia. Carry trades and stock strategies usually make some money while failing that bar, so where the chance of any profit at all differs, both figures appear. The Kalshi scores carry over from the earlier Kalshi research, which measured the same thing, a real and lasting edge after fees at a small automated desk's scale, except for the changes noted below.

The Kalshi research found that every lasting edge names someone who loses for a reason that outlasts the fee: recreational takers who overpay for long shots, or Kalshi's own budget for resting liquidity. The same test sorts every other venue, and three kinds of counterparty pass it. The first is investors paying to shed a risk: holders of takeover targets who sell rather than wait out a deal, buyers of crash insurance, sellers of neglected closed-end funds, and hedgers in futures. The second is a venue or operator paying for depth or customers, such as Polymarket US's incentive pool and the sportsbooks' sign-up budgets. The third is a thin second venue whose quotes lag the main book, which is the whole case for ForecastEx and Polymarket US gaps. Strategies that must out-forecast a liquid price or out-race professionals fail on every venue, as they did on Kalshi.

The base rates for retail traders are grim. Fewer than **1% of Taiwanese day traders** earned predictable positive returns after fees, and the group as a whole lost money in every one of 15 years ([Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). Of Brazilian futures day traders who persisted more than 300 days, **97% lost money** ([Chague et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/spawpaper/2019wpecon47.htm)). Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe ratios explained less than 2.5% of the variation in live results ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)), and published stock anomalies lost 58% of their returns after publication ([McLean & Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). The studies agree that costs, chiefly spreads and overpaying for options, cause most retail losses. That is why every strategy that survives below either rests orders or trades rarely.

The evidence carries the same warning as the Kalshi work. The four research teams behind this report were blocked from many primary pages, including Polymarket US's documentation, Interactive Brokers, Coinbase, FINRA and arXiv, so many facts come from search-engine summaries of the cited pages. Published fee schedules conflict for Coinbase, Kraken, Gemini and Polymarket US, so the bot should read every fee from the venue at runtime before any order logic depends on it. No strategy on any venue has an audited small-account track record, and the positive public claims are almost all vendor marketing. Where researchers scored the same mechanism differently, this report moves toward the low end when the support is self-reported or depends on a perk the venue has since withdrawn.

The top of the table holds two different kinds of edge. Kalshi's entries are microstructure edges: they earn cents per contract from recreational flow and from Kalshi's subsidy, and they live or die by Kalshi policy and by an Arizona court. The new entries at the same scores are risk premia: they earn a few percent a year for holding a risk others want to shed, and they live or die by how often markets crash. Merger arbitrage ties incentive capture at 35%, but a merger book that misses the bar still probably makes money (a 60% chance of some profit), while a failed incentive program bleeds to informed takers. The prediction-market newcomers rank below Kalshi's fade but above every Kalshi strategy that forecasts against the price, because they need no forecast, only a slow second book.

Three things move in Kalshi's own ranking. The cross-venue maker hybrid, scored 10% in the Kalshi research, rises to 20% because a maker leg on Polymarket US halves the fee hurdle. It stops short of the venue team's 25% because the one live record of the idea earned **$52.53 in rebates and $11.36 in rewards against $4.75 from trading** over 44 days ([Tanuj-07](https://github.com/Tanuj-07/polymarket-market-making)), and on October 19 Polymarket US replaced its flat maker rebate with tiers that pay nothing below $10 million of monthly maker volume ([Polymarket US fees](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees)). Slow Kalshi–Polymarket convergence (8%) gives way to the ForecastEx version, whose position coupon offsets most of the cost of capital locked until settlement. And the desk's own check of the 15-minute contract against the hourly ladder found identical settlement values in all 472 top-of-hour pairs over ten days, but only one quote crossing in a week that cleared taker fees, worth about **$2.56 at 100 contracts** ([Kalshi API](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/batch-get-market-candlesticks)). The sports slice of the long-shot fade drops to about 20% for an Arizona resident, for the legal reason set out below.

### Slow risk premia lead the new entries, and each is paid for crash risk

Merger arbitrage is the best-documented edge a small bot can run anywhere outside Kalshi. Across 4,750 deals from 1963 to 1998, its returns resembled selling uncovered index puts: uncorrelated with the market in flat or rising years, positively correlated in severe declines, and worth **about 4% a year over cash after transaction costs against 10.3% before them** ([Mitchell & Pulvino](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/mitchell-pulvino-characteristics-of-risk-and-return-JF.pdf)). The premium persists. The HFRI merger-arbitrage index gained 8.2% in the first nine months of 2025, its best start since 2021 ([LPL Research](https://www.lpl.com/research/blog/merger-arbitrage-rebound.html)), on deal flow and "remarkably few" breaks ([AllianceBernstein](https://www.alliancebernstein.com/americas/en/institutions/insights/investment-insights/merger-arbitrage-riding-the-wave-into-2026.html)), and one manager's team returned 12.0% net in 2025 ([Associated Capital](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/28282/associated-capital-group-inc-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-results)). Latency is irrelevant, the data is free from EDGAR filings and daily prices, and positions are small enough that 10–20 cash deals fit a few-thousand-dollar book. The score stops at 35% because rolling returns have declined since 1995 ([Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/merger-arbitrage-arbitraged-away/)), 2025's strength rested on rare breaks that can return, and one broken deal in a 10–20 position book erases the spread from many completed ones.

Closed-end fund discounts mean-revert, and small size helps here, because the funds are too small for large arbitrageurs. A long-short version earned 17.3–18.2% a year in a 1998–2011 backtest ([Quantpedia](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=563)), though the figures appear to be gross and the authors warn that higher-turnover variants are unlikely to survive costs ([vLex abstract](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/exploiting-closedend-fund-discounts-855659869)). No after-cost evidence from after 2011 turned up, discounts blow out in crises, and the free source of daily fund NAVs is unverified.

Trend following has the longest record of any strategy in this report: time-series momentum explains most managed-futures returns ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=24372)). Its recent record is thin. The SG Trend Index compounds at **4.88% a year with a 20.61% maximum drawdown**, and stood about 10% down for 2025 at the end of July ([Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-july-2025/)). Rob Carver is reported to put the minimum for a diversified futures trend system near $100,000 ([The Algorithmic Advantage](https://thealgorithmicadvantage.substack.com/p/033-rob-carver-the-comprehensive)), so at $1k–$25k whole-contract sizing leaves only a handful of micro futures and little diversification. Its main value is as crisis protection for the rest of the program.

Index put-writing earns the insurance premium honestly but not cheaply. Cboe's PutWrite index returned 9.40% a year against 9.91% for the S&P 500 from 1986 to 2023, with about two-thirds of the volatility ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/generating-income-and-managing-risk-cash-secured-put-writing-in-a-low-equity-return-environment)). It trailed the index by 7–12 points a year in 2023–2025 ([PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/^PUT/^SP500TR)) and fell about 29–30% in March 2020 ([PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/symbol/%5EPUT)). On August 5, 2024, the top short-volatility funds lost $4.1 billion ([Nasdaq/Reuters](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/billions-lost-market-volatility-bet-vix-surge-hits-traders)). A small account cannot cash-secure index puts, so it must use spreads, which give back part of the premium, and static same-day option-selling rules showed no consistent profit after costs ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/0dte-strategy-outcomes)).

The remaining stock ideas are either young or worn out. Intraday SPY momentum reports 19.6% a year net of costs with a Sharpe ratio of 1.33 over 2007–2024, but in a single practitioner backtest with no independent replication ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)). It became runnable under $25,000 when FINRA's intraday margin standard replaced the pattern-day-trader rule on June 4, 2026 ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard)), although brokers may phase the change in through October 20, 2027 ([Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-05-01/finra-replaces-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-new-intraday-margin-standards/)). Overnight-only index funds closed in 2023 after returning −6.9% against +22% for the S&P 500 ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/industry-news/etf-strategy-designed-for-night-owls-gets-a-wake-up-call/240147)). The S&P 500 inclusion effect fell from 7.4% to under 1% ([Greenwood & Sammon](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4294297)), post-earnings drift vanished outside microcaps by 2006 ([Martineau](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)), and the pre-FOMC drift disappeared after 2015 ([Kurov et al.](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf)). US retail forex is capped at 50:1 leverage on major pairs ([OANDA](https://cloudflare-www.oanda.com/us-en/trading/spreads-margin)), and only about 38% of one large broker's accounts were profitable in a quarter once interest-only accounts were excluded ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/brokers/oandas-high-profitability-numbers-explained-include-interest/)).

Broker choice decides how well these fit the house rules. Interactive Brokers is the only broker found that documents a reduced-permission username without withdrawal access, set up by request ([IBKR docs](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/general/market-data-subscriptions/market-data-users/multiple-users)), and its US data bundle costs about $10 a month, waived above $30 a month in commissions ([dev.to guide](https://dev.to/xqliu/interactive-brokers-market-data-subscription-which-one-do-i-actually-need-in-2026-164g)). Alpaca gives free paper accounts with a $100,000 default balance and free basic data, and charges $99 a month for real-time options data ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/about-market-data-api)). Schwab's API reportedly needs an interactive re-login every seven days, a poor fit for an unattended bot ([schwabr](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/schwabr/readme/README.html)). For all of these strategies paper fills are close to real, but the edge is a premium for rare losses, so a paper week tests only plumbing and costs; only a multi-year historical backtest tests the edge.

### A second prediction-market venue is cheap to test but rarely free money

Only two prediction-market venues besides Kalshi offer a bot a usable route. Polymarket US is a CFTC-designated exchange with a retail API: keys are issued after identity verification, and no retail withdrawal endpoint turned up in its documentation ([Polymarket US API](https://docs.polymarket.us/institutional/introduction); [Transfers](https://docs.polymarket.us/partners/funding/transfers)). Interactive Brokers' TWS API trades ForecastEx contracts at **$0.01 per contract per side with no IBKR commission**, and the same API reaches Kalshi and CME event contracts ([IBKR API](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-api-page/event-trading/); [kairos](https://kairos.trade/compare/forecastex-review)). ForecastEx contracts can only be bought; a position is closed by buying the opposite side, which IBKR nets automatically. Gemini Predictions copies Kalshi's fee formula ([Gemini fees](https://www.gemini.com/fees/predictions)) and says it opened its API to AI agents ([Gemini Q1 2026 call](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/gemini-space-station-inc-class-a-common-stock-nasdaqgemi-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript-1765711/)), but its API terms and Arizona availability are unverified. PredictIt has no trading API and bans bots ([eventtradinghub](https://eventtradinghub.com/predictit-guide/)). That locks away the best-documented mispricing in the field: bids on linked contracts that "often persistently added to more than 110" ([Zitzewitz, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35845)). Robinhood, Webull, Coinbase, Crypto.com's OG app and the newer sports exchanges offer no retail API that the research found, and Polymarket's international exchange would need a VPN and an on-chain wallet, so it is excluded.

Polymarket US's Arizona status needs checking at signup. One guide says the company told it in writing on October 23 that it serves every state except Nevada ([predictionmarketspicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/learn/is-polymarket-legal-in-arizona)), while Sports Illustrated lists its sports markets as unavailable in Arizona ([SI](https://www.si.com/prediction-markets/reviews/polymarket-california)).

Fees decide which gaps can pay. Polymarket US takers pay 0.0695 × contracts × P × (1 − P) and makers pay nothing ([Polymarket US fees](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees)), against Kalshi's 0.07 taker rate ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)). ForecastEx's flat fee is cheaper than Kalshi's between about 8¢ and 92¢ ([marketmath](https://marketmath.io/compare/forecastex-vs-kalshi)). Per contract, before any rounding, the gap needed to lock a profit is:

| YES price | Taker on both legs (Kalshi + Polymarket US) | Polymarket US maker + Kalshi taker | ForecastEx + Kalshi taker |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50¢ | 3.49¢ | 1.75¢ | 2.75¢ |
| 30¢ | 2.93¢ | 1.47¢ | 2.47¢ |
| 10¢ | 1.26¢ | 0.63¢ | 1.63¢ |
| 5¢ | 0.66¢ | 0.33¢ | 1.33¢ |

Real gaps are mostly smaller than that. Across 186 matched Kalshi–Polymarket pairs the median gap was **0.78¢** and only 1.5% reached 5¢, though the same dataset logs 385 sustained episodes of 2¢ or more ([PredictMarketCap](https://predictmarketcap.com/analysis/prediction-market-arbitrage-study)). On October 21, a Democratic-control midterm contract traded at 86.5¢ on Kalshi, 86¢ on Polymarket and 85.8¢ on Polymarket US ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/odds/2026-midterms/)). Locks with a taker on both legs therefore rarely pay. The tradable set is gaps of 2¢ or more in the tails, where fees shrink, and gaps where one leg rests as a maker. No one has measured gaps between Kalshi and ForecastEx or Gemini, which makes that the cheapest missing evidence in this report.

Locked capital is the second cost. Both legs stay fully collateralized until settlement, so a 1¢ net edge earns about 12% annualized over 30 days but only 4% over 90 days and 1% over a year, below cash for anything long-dated. ForecastEx's coupon of **3.38% a year on position value** ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/predictionmarkets/en/home.php)) offsets most of that cost, and because IBKR routes Kalshi contracts too, one account can hold both legs without moving money between venues. Whether IBKR adds a commission on Kalshi legs is unknown.

Rule mismatches are the third cost. Kalshi settled a Cardi B halftime-show market at its last traded price, 26¢ YES, while Polymarket paid YES at $1, and Kalshi settles viewership markets on the source's first print even when a revision crosses the line ([Oddsshopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-vs-polymarket-settlement-rules)). Every pair therefore needs a rules map a human has approved. Election markets carry an extra hazard for anyone quoting them: CNBC reported suspicious trading in election contracts on October 30 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/gamble-on-democracy-inside-suspicious-election-contracts-trading-on-prediction-markets-.html)).

Polymarket US's own incentive pool works like Kalshi's. Each second, a resting order scores its size times 0.30 for every tick it sits behind the best price, pools are split pro rata, and there is no per-person cap ([Polymarket US docs](https://docs.polymarket.us/incentives/liquidity)). Pool sizes were not visible to the researchers. On Polymarket's international venue the farming record is poor: one fork of the best-known open-source quoter warns that "in today's market, this bot is not profitable and will lose money" ([itgoyo/poly-maker](https://github.com/itgoyo/poly-maker)), and a developer who ran a clone live netted a loss ([dev.to](https://dev.to/fatherson/i-cloned-a-polymarket-market-making-bot-and-ran-it-heres-what-i-learned-46ag)). The pool is best treated as a fee offset for a quoting strategy that already breaks even. Speed limits the rest: CME fed funds futures reacted about 0.6 seconds before Kalshi after September's CPI release ([BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/when-kalshi-and-fed-funds-futures-disagree)), so news-driven gaps belong to faster firms, and only gaps that persist for minutes or hours in thin books are ours.

The long-shot fade travels to Polymarket US, where makers pay nothing and the taker fee in the tails is tiny, about 0.33¢ a contract at 5¢ ([Polymarket US fees](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees)). The venue team scored this copy at 30%. This report puts it at 25%, because no study covers Polymarket US itself, and on Polymarket's international venue long shots that lost 6.3¢ per dollar when counted contract by contract gained 4.1¢ once related contracts were grouped by event ([Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12878)). That is a warning that a ladder of NO bids on one event is one correlated bet. The copy's value is extra capacity for F, not a new edge.

### One pending Arizona ruling could switch off the Kalshi tier

Kalshi's availability in Arizona rests on a federal preliminary injunction issued on May 5, 2026, which bars the state from enforcing its gambling laws against contracts listed on CFTC-regulated exchanges and paused its criminal case against Kalshi ([Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/158205/arizona-judge-grants-cftc-preliminary-injunction-kalshi-case)). On August 28 the Ninth Circuit, which covers Arizona, held in a Nevada case that sports event contracts are likely not swaps, so state gaming law is not preempted, and it sent election contracts back to the lower court ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/28/25-7516.pdf)). In September Arizona's attorney general asked the Ninth Circuit to dissolve the Arizona injunction on the strength of that ruling ([AZ Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/09/25/after-9th-circuit-ruling-mayes-pushes-to-revive-her-gambling-case-against-kalshi/)), and no decision had been reported. The Third Circuit ruled the other way, New Jersey has asked the Supreme Court to resolve the split ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/law/analysis/2026/kalshi-third-circuit-new-jersey-scotus-1234889561/)), and Utah filed 13 criminal charges against Kalshi on November 3 ([SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/northamerica/2026/11/03/kalshi-utah-lawsuit/)).

Sports contracts are the most exposed: after the Nevada ruling, platforms could no longer offer sports contracts in Nevada, and an adverse Arizona ruling could end sports trading for Arizona users with little notice. That risk is why the sports slice of the long-shot fade drops to about 20% here. Liquidity-incentive capture is less exposed, because the pools live on November 3 sat mostly in election-day, "Trump says", Rotten Tomatoes and YouTube-view markets rather than sports. Whether an individual Arizona trader, as opposed to the operator, faces any exposure is unresolved. The design rules follow directly: keep Kalshi positions short-dated, cap long-dated sports futures, and never route around a future Arizona geofence through an out-of-state server. The desk, its paper test and all six lab strategies sit on this one venue, which is the strongest single reason to put a non-Kalshi strategy in the lab.

## Crypto carry and arbitrage now pay less than T-bills

Crypto offers the widest choice of legal venues and the least edge. An Arizona resident can trade spot on Coinbase Advanced, Kraken, Gemini, Crypto.com, Bitstamp and Binance.US, which lists Arizona as supported ([Binance.US](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9842798-list-of-supported-and-unsupported-states-and-regions)). US-regulated perpetual-style futures now trade on Coinbase Financial Markets, with hourly funding and fees "as low as 0.02%" with a $0.15 minimum per contract ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/perpetual-futures-have-arrived-in-the-us)); on Kraken, listed on Bitnomial since June 15 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260615010932/en/Kraken-Launches-Perpetual-Futures-for-US-Clients)); on Crypto.com's OG platform since October 27, at up to 5x with fees waived at launch ([Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/10/28/crypto-perpetual-contracts-us/)); and on Kalshi, whose BTCPERP launched June 3 with no trading fees during its launch period ([Bankless Times](https://www.banklesstimes.com/articles/2026/06/04/kalshi-launches-first-u-s-regulated-bitcoin-perpetuals/)). CME is suing to vacate that Kalshi approval ([Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/fctm-breaking-news-cme-sues-cftc-over-approval-of-bitcoin-perpetual-futures-contract-fctm)). CME's micro bitcoin and ether futures have traded around the clock since May 29 ([CME Group](https://investor.cmegroup.com/news-releases/news-release-details/cme-group-announces-launch-247-cryptocurrency-futures-and)) and stand on the most settled legal ground.

Every spot venue checked issues keys that trade without withdrawing: Kraken's permissions are granular, Gemini's default Trader role cannot withdraw, and Binance.US has separate toggles ([Kraken](https://docs.kraken.com/exchange/guides/rest/api-keys.md); [Gemini](https://docs.gemini.com/roles); [Binance.US](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9842812-binance-us-api-keys-best-practices-safety-tips)). Coinbase is the catch. Its single `transfer` scope covers withdrawals and also moving funds between portfolios and sweeping futures balances ([Coinbase](https://docs.cloud.coinbase.com/advanced-trade/docs/rest-api-scopes)). Unless futures margin draws on spot cash automatically, which is unverified, even a hedge held entirely at Coinbase needs the owner to top up margin by hand. Whether Kraken's and Crypto.com's US perps can be reached by API at all is also unconfirmed.

Carry, the textbook delta-neutral trade, has shrunk to about the cash rate. Average perp funding fell from **11% in 2024 to 4.9% in 2025 and 2.2% in 2026** through August 11 ([CoinDesk, citing Ethena](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/08/28/ethena-looks-beyond-crypto-to-squeeze-yield-from-booming-equity-perpetuals)). CME's 30-day annualized basis peaked at 15.3% in January 2025 ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/cme-bitcoin-futures-slump-as-basis-trade-unwinds-and-wall-street-steps-back/)), was about 5% in April 2026 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-04-09-cme-bitcoin-futures-activity-slumps-to-14-month-low-as-basis-trade-unwind-drains-institutional-demand-396722)), and by August the three-month basis of about 3% sat below the two-year Treasury ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/08/10/a-rare-cme-shift-hedge-funds-abandon-structural-shorts-to-bet-on-a-bitcoin-rally)). One October reading of Coinbase's BTC funding, +0.0006% an hour, annualizes to about 5.3% ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/futures/BIP-20DEC30-CDE)). Carry is paid by trend-chasing small investors, and the BIS finds that high carry predicts crashes, when margin spikes and liquidations make the trade dangerous ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry)).

The arithmetic on $10,000 shows the gap to cash. Held at Coinbase as $8,000 of spot BTC against an $8,000 short perp with $2,000 of margin, funding of 2.2–5.3% grosses about $176–$424 a year. Spot maker fees of 0.35–0.50% each way under the September 2026 tiers ([Altrady](https://www.altrady.com/blog/crypto-trading-platforms/coinbase-advanced-trade)) cost about $56–$80 per round trip, leaving **about $90–$365, or 0.9–3.7%, against $375–$400 in T-bills**. Splitting the legs across venues cuts spot fees to near zero on Binance.US ([Binance.US](https://blog.binance.us/zero-fee-trading/)) but strands margin. No key can move money between venues, so each short must survive a rally on its own. The starting equity it needs is N × [m(1 + x) + x], where N is the short's size, m the maintenance fraction and x the rally. At CME's 35% maintenance ([CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/how-to-trade-micro-bitcoin-futures)), surviving a 30% rally takes about 76% of notional in margin, roughly **$14,000 of capital per micro bitcoin contract**. Carry on total capital is then the carry rate divided by one plus that ratio, so 4% carry with margin at 60% of notional yields 2.5%. To beat cash, funding has to run at about 6% a year. 2024's average would have cleared that; 2025's and 2026's would not.

So the plain answer for crypto today is cash. Every always-on carry trade has a 50–60% chance of making some money and only about a 10% chance of beating T-bills. The sensible form is a registered switch: log funding and basis on every US venue, and open the hedged trade only when funding runs above about 6% a year, or CME basis sits at least 5 points over cash as it did in January 2025, when the chance of beating cash is roughly even. Funding spikes work only as a filter, not a signal, since an independent study of 2023–2026 data finds crowded-long reversals "at best a faint tailwind" ([Bryan Vine](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper2.html)).

Arbitrage and market making are fee-blocked at our size. BTC and ETH spreads averaged **0.12 and 0.11 basis points** in January 2026 ([Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/digital-asset-snapshot-2026-rally-etf-inflows-positioning-shifts?page=30)), while a two-venue taker trade needs a 1.15–1.70% gap between Coinbase and Kraken, or 0.42–0.82% with a Binance.US leg. Zero maker fees exist only on Binance.US and Gemini's stablecoin pairs ([Gemini](https://gemini.com/fees/activetrader-fee-schedule)); Coinbase reaches 0% maker only around $50 million of monthly volume. Binance.US has the widest US spreads and the most outliers ([Kaiko](https://research.kaiko.com/insights/a-cheatsheet-for-bid-ask-spreads)) on "abysmal" volume ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/370097/crypto-exchange-binance-us-cuts-fees-as-trading-volumes-remain-abysmal)), so its wide spreads mostly price the risk of trading against better-informed flow, and the one small market-making run with published numbers lost about 1.2% ([Hummingbot](https://hummingbot.org/academy-content/sharing-is-caring-3-trading-pairs-i-picked/)). Without withdrawals, inventory rebalances only when a gap reverses, so capacity equals idle capital pre-positioned on each venue.

Two exceptions earn a place on a watch list rather than in the lab. Crashes open real gaps: in October 2025, BTC prices across major exchanges differed by more than **$4,000 within minutes** ([Kaiko](https://kaiko.com/news/prediction-markets-face-a-pricing-infrastructure-problem)), far beyond any fee, but the capital waits idle between such events and the APIs tend to fail during them. Fee holidays on the new perp venues lower every hurdle to near zero, but onshore perp volume is estimated at about 0.15% of offshore ([MarketTrace](https://markettrace.ai/blog/regulated-us-perps-order-flow)), and the promotions end. Calendar and listing effects fail on evidence: the session that led bitcoin's gains flipped within a year ([KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-gains-focused-on-north-american-trading-hours-in-2026-asian-session-weighs-on-performance)), CME's weekend gap disappeared with round-the-clock trading, and abnormal returns that build before a new exchange listing turn negative from the first day after it ([LIPIcs AFT 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol354-aft2025/html/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.11/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.11.html)). One lead is too new to score: on November 3 the CFTC let stock-linked perps for US venues be deemed approved ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-equity-perpetual-futures-trading-us-exchanges)), while offshore equity perps were paying funding of about 14–17.5% ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/08/28/ethena-looks-beyond-crypto-to-squeeze-yield-from-booming-equity-perpetuals)).

## Arizona sportsbooks pay people, not bots

The most dependable money in this report sits at Arizona's sportsbooks, and none of it can be automated. About 13–15 books hold Arizona Department of Gaming licenses, among them FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, Caesars, bet365, Fanatics, BetRivers, Hard Rock Bet, Desert Diamond and theScore Bet ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/betting/usa/arizona)). Circa holds a license and is targeting an early-2027 launch ([Rotowire](https://www.rotowire.com/news/circa-sports-approved-to-launch-in-arizona-126207)). Arizona has no licensed betting exchange. Sporttrade closed its US sportsbook operations in mid-2026 ([Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/05/19/121042-sporttrade-to-exit-us-online-sports-betting-markets-while-awaiting-cftc-decision)), the state ordered ProphetX and BetOpenly to cease operating ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/231261/illegal-operators-arizona-sports-betting-cease-and-desists-apr-2025/); [Lines.com](https://www.lines.com/arizona-orders-five-gambling-sites-to-shut-down)), and Crypto.com withdrew its sports contracts from Arizona in December 2025 ([SportsBettingDime](https://www.sportsbettingdime.com/news/betting/crypto-com-no-longer-offers-sport-event-contracts-in-arizona-eight-other-states/)). Offshore books and sweepstakes apps are excluded.

Two rules keep the bot from placing bets. FanDuel's terms ban robots, scrapers and "sniping" software ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/fanduel/fanduel-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-043908/prohibition-on-unauthorized-automated-access/)), and DraftKings' daily-fantasy terms ban automated interaction with its site ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/draftkings/draftkings-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-039377/automated-means-to-interact-with-website-prohibited/)). Arizona also requires every wager to come from a device located in the state ([Cornell LII, R19-4-117](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R19-4-117)), which a cloud server elsewhere fails and which we will not fake. Every sportsbook strategy is therefore "bot computes, person taps". The bot can scan a licensed odds feed, remove the bookmaker's margin, size bets, log results and place a Kalshi hedge by API once a person confirms the book accepted the bet; it should not scrape the books themselves.

The sign-up sweep is the one high-confidence item, at **85% to net positive before tax**. Arizona's welcome offers include bet365's $200 in bonus bets for a $5 bet, BetMGM's up to $1,500 back in bonus bets if the first bet loses, and FanDuel's $250 in bonus bets ([Rotowire](https://www.rotowire.com/betting/arizona-betting-promos)). Hedged bonus bets typically convert to cash at 65–75% ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/education/bonus-bet-conversion)), and Whelan's model puts the best conversion at "low, but not very low" win probabilities ([Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/FreeBets.pdf)). In a worked example, a $200 bonus bet at +300 hedged with 600 Kalshi NO contracts at 75¢ costs $457.88 and locks $142.12, or 71%, against about $130 when hedged at another book. Across about a dozen books, the sweep is worth roughly **$1.5k–$3k before tax, once**, on mid-2026 terms that need rechecking. Reload promotions add perhaps a few hundred dollars a month at first and fade as books restrict promo-only accounts (55%).

Betting soft books against sharp prices is the best-evidenced recurring method, at 55% before tax. Kaunitz, Zhong and Kreiner bet wherever a book's odds beat the consensus with the margin removed, made **5.5% in live paper trading** ($1,128.50 on 407 bets of $50), then staked real money until bookmakers restricted them within weeks to months ([Kaunitz et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)). Beating the closing line validates the method within a few hundred bets, so the bot can log closing-line value on paper before anyone stakes a dollar. Winners get limited, though. In Massachusetts, 0.64% of bettors were limited, and 57.6% of those were cut to 1–24% of the normal maximum ([SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/09/30/mgc-data-limiting-bettors-ma/)). A sharp reference now costs money, since Pinnacle closed its public API in July 2025 ([Pinnacle](https://github.com/pinnacleapi/pinnacleapi-documentation)) and The Odds API refreshes featured markets only every 40–60 seconds ([The Odds API](https://the-odds-api.com/)). Building a better model does not help in the main markets: return predictability in betting prices is a fraction of that in financial markets and fails to cover costs ([Moskowitz](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:76:y:2021:i:6:p:3153-3209)).

Arbitrage and middles are rarer and shorter than they look. Live NBA arbitrage between FanDuel and BetMGM existed in **4.52% of game time and typically lasted about 13 seconds** ([Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/437f7731-67ad-4af7-a1e6-648c1d003d46)). Bookmakers' account management stops informed bettors from exploiting such prices ([Grant et al.](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/49935)), and no liquid game cleared fees against Kalshi or Polymarket, where in-game gaps lasted about 6 seconds ([sportstrades](https://github.com/VV1Git/sportstrades)). Middles across key numbers pay when they appear: NFL games end on a margin of exactly 3 about 15% of the time ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/nfl-margin-of-victory-data-1999-2025)), so a full point across 3 at standard prices is worth roughly +10%, but such splits are rare and flag the account (25%).

Taxes decide whether any of it pays. From 2026, federal law lets bettors deduct only 90% of gambling losses, and only if they itemize ([Clarity Tax Counsel](https://claritytaxcounsel.com/guides/90-percent-gambling-loss-rule)). A repeal cleared the House Ways and Means Committee 38–5 on September 16 but is not law ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/house-committee-advances-repeal-of-90-percent-gambling-loss-deduction-limit-sept-16-2026)), and Arizona taxes winnings at 2.5% ([Sovos](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/trr/arizona-lowers-gambling-withholding-rate-to-2-5/)). On even-money bets the disallowed tenth of losses works out to taxable phantom income of about 5% of turnover, so at a 24.5% combined marginal rate (22% federal plus 2.5% Arizona) any edge below about **1.6% of turnover loses money after tax** even for an itemizer. Most arbitrage falls below that. A filer who takes the standard deduction fares worse, because losses then deduct nothing. A single filer whose other itemized deductions total about $4,000, with $15,000 of gross winnings, $12,000 of losses and $3,000 of real profit, still takes the standard deduction, pays tax on the full $15,000, about $3,700, and ends roughly $700 down. How Kalshi's sports contracts are taxed is unresolved ([CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax)), so a hedge whose legs land in different tax baskets needs a CPA's review before it is scaled.

The sportsbooks therefore belong outside the paper lab. They offer a one-time manual project worth a few thousand dollars before tax and a few months of human-placed value bets before limits arrive, both worth doing only after a tax plan. The bot's legitimate part is the calculator, the closing-line log and the Kalshi hedge leg, while Kalshi's Arizona status lasts.

## Add a gap recorder and a merger book to the lab on November 10

The lab exists to settle uncertainty before money moves, so each addition should pass three tests. It needs a named counterparty, a paper test that can kill or bound it within weeks, and a fit with the house rules: keys that trade but cannot withdraw, no on-chain wallets, and the owner approving every key. Two candidates pass all three, and a third is a logger that trades nothing. Each should be registered when the paper week ends on November 10, and its forward results should count only data recorded after registration, as A–F do.

| Proposed | Strategy | Venues and data | Honest scoring | Bar for going further | Owner action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G | Cross-venue prediction-market gaps | Kalshi through the existing recorder; ForecastEx quotes and sizes through IBKR's TWS API; Polymarket US once an account clears identity checks; a rules map a human approves for each pair | Gap size, duration and depth net of each venue's exact fee; taker fills at displayed size after a realistic delay; maker legs bounded pessimistically, as in C, E and F | Gaps that clear fees with real size, outlast the bot's delay and recur weekly earn a tiny live pilot | Open an IBKR account, request a username without withdrawal rights, sign the market-data API acknowledgement |
| H | Merger arbitrage | EDGAR filings for announced cash deals, free daily prices, a free Alpaca paper account | First a historical backtest over every eligible cash deal of recent years, with costs and breaks; then forward paper fills at the far side of the quote | A backtest that beats T-bills after costs and breaks across most years, not just 2025, plus paper costs matching the model, earns a small live book | None to start; an IBKR or Alpaca live account later |
| I (optional) | US crypto funding and basis logger, trading nothing | Funding prints from Coinbase Financial Markets, Kalshi BTCPERP and, where readable, Kraken and Crypto.com/OG; CME basis | A pre-registered switch opens paper carry only above about 6% a year of funding, or CME basis 5 points over cash | Several weeks of paper carry beating cash after fees and stranded margin goes to the owner for a decision | A view-only Coinbase key; Kalshi's existing market-data key, if it covers perps (unverified) |

G comes first because it is the closest to the desk's existing code and resolves the widest uncertainty in this report. It reuses the recorder, the pessimistic maker fills and the fee logic already built for A–F. Its 25% score could rise above 40% or fall below 10% within a month of recorded quotes, and its taker legs are honest on paper. Holding both legs in one IBKR account sidesteps the no-withdrawal treasury problem that cripples cross-venue crypto, and ForecastEx's coupon offsets most of the cost of capital locked until settlement. It should start with Fed, CPI, jobs and climate contracts, the markets least exposed to the Arizona dispute. The Polymarket US maker leg and its incentive pool come second, once the taker map shows where gaps live. Three questions remain open: whether IBKR charges a commission on Kalshi legs, how deep ForecastEx's books are, and whether an IBKR paper account can read ForecastEx quotes.

H comes second because it ties the top score, stands on settled legal ground, ignores latency, runs on free data and fails for reasons unrelated to Kalshi or the Arizona ruling. A paper week cannot confirm a premium paid for rare deal breaks, so the historical backtest is the real test, and the forward paper book checks only plumbing, costs and the deal screen. The rules should be written down before the backtest runs. Cash deals only; stock deals, which need a short of the acquirer, come later. Positions should be equal in size, deals facing heavy regulatory review skipped, and each position held until the deal completes or breaks.

I is optional because carry loses to cash today, but it is the cheapest insurance against missing the next boom like January 2025's. No history of US-venue funding exists, the logger trades nothing until its switch fires, and carry is one of the few strategies paper judges honestly, because funding and basis are public and fills do not depend on queue position.

E and F should keep running, since they remain Kalshi's strongest candidates. A–D's results after November 10 decide their future: A settles whether the repaired model deserves any weight, and the pair check already suggests D's top-of-hour pair adds little. Five candidates wait their turn. Closed-end fund reversion and micro-futures trend following score 30% but need fund NAV or futures history and years of results, so they follow once H's backtest machinery exists. Index put spreads wait because crash risk is invisible on paper and historical options chains cost from about $50 one-off to about $300 a month ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/flashalpha-vs-orats-options-data-api-backtesting)). The Polymarket US long-shot fade adds capacity to F rather than a new edge, so it waits until F's bound looks positive. Sportsbook strategies stay out of the lab entirely, because the bot cannot place the bets. Until a test passes, idle capital belongs in T-bills, where it earns the bar every strategy has to beat.

## Conclusion

Widening the search from Kalshi to every legal venue changes the shape of the opportunity, not its size. On every venue the edges that survive are paid by someone shedding a risk or by a venue buying customers and depth, and none tops 35%. What the other venues add is independence. Kalshi's edges fail if Kalshi trims its incentive budget, which it amended at least three times this year ([CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07152610358.pdf)), or if an Arizona ruling goes against it. Merger arbitrage fails in crashes and waves of broken deals, and cross-venue gaps fail if the second books deepen. No single score improves, but a program spread across failure modes that do not coincide is far more likely to have something working a year from now. The binding constraints also turned out to be rules rather than speed. The no-withdrawal key strands margin in cross-venue crypto, sportsbook terms and geofencing keep the bot from betting, and the 2026 tax rule turns thin betting margins into losses. That makes the choice of broker, one Interactive Brokers account with a no-withdrawal user reaching four asset classes, worth more than any single strategy in this report.

The honest default is cash at about 4%, and the lab's job is to find a strategy that earns its way out of it. These scores are perishable in both directions. Polymarket US rewrote its rebates on October 19, US funding rates could return to 2024's levels, the Ninth Circuit could rule on Arizona any week, and the cap on gambling-loss deductions could be repealed retroactively. Each of those would re-rank the table, so the ranking should be re-scored whenever one of them moves, not on a calendar.

## The short version

Nothing on the other legal platforms beats Kalshi's two best ideas, and nothing anywhere is a sure thing. The best odds of a real edge are about one in three.

Crypto exchanges: the "safe" trade that collects funding payments now earns less than T-bills, which pay about 4% a year. Keep that money in cash until those payments jump.

Sportsbooks: sign-up bonuses are real money, roughly $1,500 to $3,000 once, before tax. But a person in Arizona has to place every bet, the books ban bots, and the 2026 tax rule can turn the profit into a loss if you do not itemize. See a tax preparer first.

Stocks and futures: slow, boring strategies have the best long record, especially buying shares of companies that are being bought out. They win by taking crash risk and take years to prove.

Other prediction markets: no one has yet checked whether ForecastEx, reached through Interactive Brokers, prices the same events differently from Kalshi often enough to profit. Checking costs very little.

After November 10, add two tests to the paper lab: a price-gap recorder between Kalshi and ForecastEx, and a paper merger-arbitrage portfolio. A crypto funding-rate logger is an optional third. No real money goes in until a test passes.

One Interactive Brokers account, set up so it can trade but not withdraw, covers most of this.
