# Itemizing decides whether Arizona's bonus sweep pays

Converting Arizona's sportsbook sign-up bonuses into cash works. The eleven welcome offers live on November 7 are worth about **$1.6k–$1.9k before tax** to someone who can tie up **$2.5k–$3k for about five weeks** and spend **12–20 hours** placing bets. With $5k or more behind the two big refund offers, the figure rises to about $2.3k. The method is mechanical: put each bonus bet on an underdog at the promo book, bet the favorite with cash elsewhere, and keep 65–72% of the bonus whichever side wins. Kalshi converts 3–6 points better than a second sportsbook, but it stands on a federal injunction Arizona is asking the Ninth Circuit to dissolve. Whether the sweep is worth doing turns on one line of the owner's tax return. In 2026 every winning bet is taxable, while losing bets deduct only on an itemized return and only at 90%, and hedging manufactures winnings: the sweep generates about **$5.9k of gross winnings to net $1.6k**. An itemizer keeps about **$1.1k–$1.3k** after federal and Arizona tax. A non-itemizer in the 22% bracket keeps **about $200–$350**, and loses money on BetMGM's $1,500 refund offer, the largest on the board. For a non-itemizer the better play is seven bet-and-get offers with the bonus bets left unhedged, worth about **$700 after tax** on a few hundred dollars of cash. Three offers close this month: FanDuel on November 22, bet365 on November 23 and DraftKings on November 29. The researchers could not open the operators' pages, so every term here comes from search-engine summaries. BetMGM's own terms list a promotion period that ended November 1, and the Arizona offers of DraftKings, Fanatics, BetRivers and theScore could not be confirmed on the operators' own pages. Dealbot can size the hedges, read Kalshi's order book and keep the calendar and tax ledger. A person in Arizona must place every bet, because of the books' terms, Arizona's per-bet geolocation rule and Dealbot's own ban on live orders.

## A $200 bonus bet converts to about $130 of cash

Every bonus bet at an Arizona book is "stake not returned": a winning bonus bet pays only the profit. FanDuel's own example is a $25 bonus bet that returns $22.73 ([FanDuel support](https://support.fanduel.com/s/article/Bonus-Bets)). DraftKings, bet365 and theScore apply the same rule ([DraftKings support](https://support.draftkings.com/dk/en-us/what-is-a-bonus-bet?id=kb_article_view&sysparm_article=KB0010380); [bet365 Arizona](https://www.bet365.com/hub/en-us/states/arizona); [theScore help](https://thescorebethelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/14848235692045-Bonus-Bets)). A bonus bet is therefore worth less than its face value, and converting it means locking in a fixed share of that face.

bet365's current offer shows the steps. Deposit $10, claim "Bet $10, Get $200 in Bonus Bets" in the app, and place a $10 qualifier at −500 or longer that settles within 30 days ([bet365 Arizona](https://www.bet365.com/hub/en-us/states/arizona)). When the $200 arrives, put it on an underdog at about +300 at bet365, such as an NBA moneyline. Then put **$469.57** of cash on the favorite at −360 at a different book. If the underdog wins, bet365 pays $600 and the hedge loses $469.57, leaving $130.43. If the favorite wins, the bonus bet costs nothing and the hedge pays $130.43. Either way the bonus has become **$130.43 of withdrawable cash, a 65.2% conversion**.

The general rule uses decimal odds, which count the stake: +300 is 4.00 and −360 is about 1.28. Take a bonus bet of size B at decimal odds o_b. Hedge it with a cash stake of B(o_b − 1)/o_h on the other side at decimal odds o_h. The hedge locks B(o_b − 1)(o_h − 1)/o_h whichever side wins. The table applies the rule to typical two-book price pairs. Its figures, like every dollar value, hedge size and after-tax number in this report, are my arithmetic on the cited terms and illustrative prices, not measured results.

| Bonus odds / hedge odds (combined margin) | Conversion | Hedge on a $200 bonus | Cash locked |
|---|---|---|---|
| +150 / −170 (3.0%) | 55.6% | $189 | $111 |
| +200 / −240 (3.9%) | 58.8% | $282 | $118 |
| +300 / −360 (3.3%) | 65.2% | $470 | $130 |
| +300 / −400 (5.0%) | 60.0% | $480 | $120 |
| +400 / −450 (1.8%, a shopped price) | 72.7% | $655 | $145 |
| +500 / −650 (3.3%) | 66.7% | $867 | $133 |
| +1000 / −1400 (2.4%) | 66.7% | $1,867 | $133 |

Conversion rises with the bonus odds, because the unreturned stake costs less on a long shot. That is why Whelan's model of hedged free bets puts the best target at "low but not very low" win probabilities ([Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/FreeBets.pdf)). Past about +500, wider margins and hedge stakes of up to nine times the bonus eat the gain. The realistic planning range is **65–72%**. Action Network calls 70% or better "good" ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/education/bonus-bet-conversion)). The 80% figures in some guides need a price pair with almost no combined margin, such as 4.00 against 1.36, which converts at 79.4% ([TopEndSports](https://www.topendsports.com/sport/betting-tools/bonus-bet-calculator.htm); [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/betting-101/how-to-convert-a-free-bet)). Shopping both legs across books matters more than any other skill: moving the hedge from −400 to −360 adds five points.

Refund offers flip the order: the hedge goes on with the first bet. BetMGM's Arizona First Bet Offer returns a losing first bet, up to $1,500, as five bonus bets. Each is worth 20% of the stake and expires after seven days ([BetMGM Arizona](https://www.az.betmgm.com/en/engage/lan/sports/first-bet-offer)). Bet $1,500 at −110 at BetMGM, and **$950** on the other side at −110 at another book.

If the BetMGM bet wins, it pays $1,363.64 against the $950 hedge, for +$413.64. If it loses, the hedge pays $863.64 against the $1,500 lost, and the five $300 tokens, converted at 70%, add $1,050: again +$413.64. The hedge stake is Q(o_q − c)/o_h, where Q is the qualifier, o_q and o_h are the two decimal prices, and c is the conversion expected on the refund.

Even-money qualifiers lock **about $374–$453 on roughly $2,450 at risk**, depending on how well the tokens convert. A +300 qualifier locks $567–$685, but it ties up about $5,400, plus $3,500 more to convert the tokens. Left unhedged, the same offer is worth about 31% of the qualifier at −110 and 50% at +300. One author puts a well-played no-sweat bet at "as much as 60%" of its size ([JuiceReel](https://juicereel.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-maximize-the-no-sweat-bet)). The hedge gives up a few points of value for certainty.

The other formats are simpler. Caesars' ten 100% profit boosts double the winnings on stakes of up to $25 and pay the extra in cash ([Caesars Arizona terms](https://www.caesars.com/sportsbook-and-casino/az/support/promotions/)). A boosted $25 at +200 becomes +400, and $88.24 on the other side at −240 locks $11.76, so the ten boosts lock about **$85–$130**. Desert Diamond matches a first deposit above $100 with up to $250 in five bonus bets, usable at −200 or longer. The deposit must be wagered once before bonus winnings can be withdrawn ([Desert Diamond help](https://help.betdesertdiamond.com/hc/en-us/articles/39783613225755--250-Welcome-Match-offer)). Converting the five $50 bets and turning the deposit over once nets **about $140–$176**. Fanatics pays in FanCash, which becomes bonus bets one-for-one but casino credit at only 50 cents on the dollar ([Fanatics FanCash terms](https://sportsbook.fanatics.com/legal/tn/fancash-program-terms/)). FanCash should therefore always go to bonus bets.

The default hedge venue is another licensed Arizona book. Two or three accounts whose welcome offers are finished, each funded with a few hundred dollars, cover most hedges. A free odds screen picks the one with the best price on the favorite. Arizona has no licensed betting exchange. Sporttrade shut its US sportsbooks in mid-2026 ([Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/05/19/121042-sporttrade-to-exit-us-online-sports-betting-markets-while-awaiting-cftc-decision)). The state ordered ProphetX and BetOpenly to stop operating ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/231261/illegal-operators-arizona-sports-betting-cease-and-desists-apr-2025/); [Lines.com](https://www.lines.com/arizona-orders-five-gambling-sites-to-shut-down)). Novig excludes Arizona ([Dimers](https://www.dimers.com/prediction-markets/novig/legal)), and Crypto.com withdrew its sports contracts from Arizona in December 2025 ([SportsBettingDime](https://www.sportsbettingdime.com/news/betting/crypto-com-no-longer-offers-sport-event-contracts-in-arizona-eight-other-states/)). Reports on whether Polymarket US offers sports in Arizona go both ways ([SI](https://www.si.com/prediction-markets/reviews/polymarket-california); [PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/learn/is-polymarket-legal-in-arizona)).

Kalshi beats a book because its fee is smaller than a book's margin on the favorite. The taker fee is 0.07 × price × (1 − price) per contract, rounded up per order ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)). At 75¢ that is 1.31¢, so the favorite costs the equivalent of **−322, against −360 or worse at a book**.

To hedge the bet365 bonus on Kalshi, buy 600 contracts on the favorite: the bonus times its decimal odds minus one. At a 75¢ ask they cost $457.88 with fees and pay $600 if the favorite wins. Either outcome then leaves **$142.12, a 71% conversion**. At a 76¢ ask the lock is $136.34, and at 77¢ it is $130.56, no better than the book. Kalshi's whole advantage is the bookmaker margin it leaves out of the hedge side. By my arithmetic that is worth roughly $50–$100 across the whole sweep, and it disappears when the book's +300 is itself shaded below fair odds. On a −110 refund qualifier, Kalshi's 50¢ contract costs about −107 all in. Because fees round up per order, each hedge should go in as one limit order.

Main game markets are deep, with a record **$4.86 billion** traded over NFL Week 1 ([PredictionNews](https://predictionnews.com/story/kalshi-posts-record-4-9-billion-football-weekend-volume)), so a $500–$2,000 hedge is small. Props and live markets are far thinner ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/prediction-markets/kalshi-review/)). Most markets settle within a few hours ([Kalshi help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823821-market-faqs)), and free ACH withdrawals take one to three business days ([KalshiView](https://kalshiview.com/blog/kalshi-withdrawal-guide-how-long-does-it-actually-take/)).

Kalshi's rules differ from a book's in ways that can break a hedge. Kalshi counts overtime. An NFL tie pays $0.50 a contract when the market has no tie outcome. A game that does not start within 48 hours settles at Kalshi's "last fair market price" instead of being voided ([Kalshi NFL game terms](https://assets.kalshi.com/contract_terms/NFLGAME.pdf); [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-postponed-game-rules)). A player who never plays settles a prop at a fair price, not a refund ([Kalshi help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/17515177-protected-nfl-player-markets)). Books typically void the last two, which leaves the Kalshi leg unhedged. The safe markets are NBA and NHL game winners, which cannot end tied, and NFL or college spreads and totals set at the same number on both venues. Hedge them close to game time, and only after the book has accepted its leg. Soccer three-way markets, props, live bets and combos stay off Kalshi.

Kalshi's legal footing in Arizona is the bigger risk. Access rests on Judge Liburdi's May 5, 2026 injunction, which bars the state from enforcing its gambling laws against contracts on federally regulated exchanges ([Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/158205/arizona-judge-grants-cftc-preliminary-injunction-kalshi-case)). On August 28 the Ninth Circuit, which covers Arizona, ruled in Nevada's case that Kalshi is unlikely to show federal law overrides state gaming law for sports contracts ([Ninth Circuit](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/28/25-7516.pdf)). In September Arizona's attorney general asked the court to dissolve the Arizona injunction ([AZ Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/09/25/after-9th-circuit-ruling-mayes-pushes-to-revive-her-gambling-case-against-kalshi/)). No ruling had surfaced by November 7.

On November 4 a federal judge barred Kalshi's sports contracts on two California tribes' lands, and Kalshi said it is using GeoComply location blocks to comply ([iGaming Express](https://igamingexpress.com/kalshi-tribal-lands-sports-contracts-injunction/)). An Arizona tribe could copy that approach. Whether an individual Arizona user faces any legal exposure was not researched. The workable rule is to hedge on Kalshi only on same-day games, so nothing is stranded if Arizona is geofenced. Fall back to book hedges the day it is, and never route around a block with a VPN or an out-of-state server.

## Books answer hedgers with promo bans, not confiscation

The terms the researchers could read all ban the same things. DraftKings' Arizona terms allow "only one (1) Account per person" and prohibit "automated means (including without limitation harvesting bots, robots, parser, spiders, or screen scrapers)" and "any type of bonus abuse" ([DraftKings Arizona terms](https://sportsbook.draftkings.com/legal/az-terms-of-use)). The indexed page dates from about 2023. FanDuel's sportsbook terms in other states limit promotions to one per person, matched on household address, IP address, phone and payment account. They let FanDuel restrict promotion eligibility based on wagering patterns, or on a failure to take a promotion in good faith ([FanDuel NJ terms](https://account.nj.casino.fanduel.com/terms)). FanDuel's Arizona version did not surface.

bet365's US terms list arbitrage as prohibited conduct ([bet365 NC terms](https://help.nc.bet365.com/s/en-us/terms-and-conditions)). A bonus hedge at fair prices is not arbitrage in the usual sense, but a book could label it so. Arizona's own rules require every operator to bar a patron from holding more than one account ([R19-4-133](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R19-4-133)); one account at each book satisfies that rule. No term the researchers found bans hedging a bonus at a *different* book. Matched-betting sites report that what gets accounts closed is multi-accounting and identity fraud ([Ungambled](https://www.ungambled.com/blog/matched-betting-bonus-hunting/bonus-abuse-vs-bonus-hunting/)).

The pattern, however, is visible. The promo book cannot see a hedge placed at a competitor, but it sees an account that bets only promotions, at maximum size, on plus-money lines. The usual response is a "promo ban", which leaves the account open with no further offers, rather than closure ([ProfitDuel](https://www.profitduel.com/blog/avoid-sportsbook-account-limited)). Regulated books mostly void winnings only for palpable pricing errors ([AVO](https://www.avo.bet/articles/is-arbitrage-betting-legal)). There are exceptions. New York's attorney general warned in 2022 that books restricted accounts for "irregular playing patterns", citing hedging, and that at least one blocked withdrawals ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/newsroom/articles/2022/kevin-thomas/consumer-alert-ag-warns-new-yorkers-deceptive-online-sports)). One review rates Caesars' bonus-abuse definitions broad enough to void promotional winnings after the fact ([terms.law](https://terms.law/ToS-Watchdog/sports-betting/caesars/)).

Stake limits mostly hit winning bettors: in Massachusetts 0.64% of bettors were limited ([SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/09/30/mgc-data-limiting-bettors-ma/)). Nobody has published how fast US books promo-ban hedgers. Massachusetts reportedly requires books to explain a restriction from June 2026 ([BettingSite.net](https://www.bettingsite.net/blog/sportsbook-limiting-winning-bettors/)), but Arizona has no such rule. A dispute goes to the operator first, then to the Arizona Department of Gaming ([ADG](https://gaming.az.gov/faq-sub-categories/complaints)). Since welcome offers come once per customer anyway, a promo ban mostly costs the thin reloads.

Five actions break an explicit clause and so turn a promo ban into a confiscation risk. The first is holding a second account at any book, including betting through a spouse's or roommate's account. The second is letting anyone, or any software, use your login. The third is betting both sides of a promotion at the same book. The fourth is spoofing your location. The fifth, at FanDuel, is withdrawing a deposit bonus straight after depositing ([FanDuel terms](https://www.fanduel.com/terms)). Because the books match on household, two adults in one home may be held to one promotion at FanDuel and BetMGM.

Most money is lost to fine print, not to the books. Qualifiers must meet odds floors: −500 or longer at bet365, Hard Rock and Fanatics, and −250 or longer at Bally. A cash-out voids the offer at Caesars, Hard Rock, FanDuel and bet365. A push does not count at bet365, so moneylines beat whole-number spreads. Bally counts only the first wager and BetMGM refunds only the first bet, so neither account should host a hedge before its qualifier. Most bonus bets expire seven days after issue, and FanCash may expire in 24 hours.

Eligibility traps catch others. theScore's reset excludes anyone who ever registered and deposited with a PENN product, and ESPN BET accounts were migrated to theScore automatically ([theScore help](https://thescorebethelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43010570595085--1-000-Bet-Reset); [Rotogrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/sports-betting/thescorebet)). Desert Diamond's match requires being in Arizona and "not on Indian Lands" ([Desert Diamond help](https://help.betdesertdiamond.com/hc/en-us/articles/39783613225755--250-Welcome-Match-offer)).

A hedge breaks when one leg is voided, rejected or repriced. Place the book leg first, add the hedge only once the book has accepted it at the expected price, and keep both legs on main markets with identical settlement rules. The last risk is behavioral: hedged promos can slide into unhedged "fun" bets. The guardrails are a fixed offer list, a fixed end date, and Arizona's statewide self-exclusion program, which covers event wagering ([ADG](https://gaming.az.gov/resources/ResponsibleGaming)).

## Hedging turns $1.6k of profit into $5.9k of taxable winnings

The 2026 federal rules are blunt. Gambling winnings are "fully taxable" whether or not a tax form arrives, and losses count only as an itemized deduction on Schedule A ([IRS Topic 419](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc419)). The 2025 tax law cut that deduction to **90% of losses, still capped at winnings** ([IRS W-2G instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw2g)). The research treats each sports wager as standing alone. A winning bet's profit is income and a losing cash stake is a loss, and losses cannot be netted against winnings with only the difference reported ([IRS Pub 529](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p529)).

A W-2G arrives only when sports winnings reach $2,000 and are at least 300 times the wager ([IRS W-2G instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw2g)). A +300 payout never meets that test, so the IRS gets no third-party report of any of this. The income is still reportable, and the ledger is the defense. House Ways and Means voted on September 16 to repeal the 90% cap retroactively to 2026 ([Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/sep/17/house-ways-and-means-committee-advances-bill-to-re/)), but no House floor vote or Senate action had followed by November 7 ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/277757/gambling-loss-deduction-fix-clears-key-hurdle/)).

Arizona taxes winnings at its flat **2.5%** rate ([Sovos](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/trr/arizona-lowers-gambling-withholding-rate-to-2-5/)). In June it adopted the federal tax code as of January 1, 2026 ([SB 1861 fact sheet](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/2R/summary/S.1861-4168ATT_ASENACTED.DOCX.htm)), so its itemized gambling deduction most likely carries the 90% cap too, though nobody has confirmed it. The researchers also could not verify whether Arizona lets someone who takes the federal standard deduction itemize on the state return. That question is worth at most about $100 here.

Phantom income comes from the hedge. When the $200 bonus bet at +300 wins, it produces $600 of income while the $469.57 hedge loses. When it loses, the hedge's $130.43 is income, and the bonus bet, which risked no money, produces no deductible loss. The bonus wins about a quarter of the time. A non-itemizer's expected taxable income is therefore **$248 on a $130 profit**, against $142 for an itemizer. At 24.5% (22% federal plus Arizona's 2.5%), the non-itemizer keeps about $70 and the itemizer about $96.

Refund offers are worse, because the qualifier and its hedge are both large cash bets. BetMGM's even-money version produces about $2,030 of expected winnings on a $378 expected profit. A non-itemizer at 24.5% ends **about $120 down** after tax, while an itemizer keeps about $245.

The table runs the whole sweep through the same model. It uses the low-end 65% conversion, hedges at other books and refund offers at even money. It shows a non-itemizer in the 12% and 22% federal brackets and an itemizer in the 22% bracket, each paying Arizona's 2.5%.

Whether one itemizes decides most of the answer. On identical bets the itemizer keeps **$1,133** and the non-itemizer in the 22% bracket **$198**. At a 73% conversion the same model gives $1,292 and $360. The bracket matters next: a non-itemizer in the 12% bracket keeps $786, and one in the 24% bracket about $80.

The test is mechanical. The sweep's expected losses are about $4.2k, and 90% of that, about $3.8k, is deductible. Someone who already itemizes gets the full deduction. So does someone whose other itemized deductions fall within about $3.8k of their standard deduction, but only partly; anyone further below gets nothing from the losses. With other deductions $2,100 short of the standard deduction, for example, only $1,718 of the $3,818 counts, and the sweep keeps about $620.

Losses are deducted below the line, so the full $5.9k of winnings also lands in adjusted gross income (AGI) for everyone. That matters to anyone whose health-insurance subsidies, Social Security taxation or other credits are pegged to AGI. The repeal fight that fills the trade press is worth about **$100** here, the tax on the disallowed tenth of the itemizer's losses. The itemizing threshold is worth about $900.

The model also points to a cheaper way for a non-itemizer to collect: skip the hedge on the bonus bets. A bonus bet risks none of your money, and unhedged it creates income only when it wins, so it generates no phantom income. Placed at about +300 and winning about 24% of the time, each dollar of bonus is worth about 72¢ before tax, and **54¢ after tax at 24.5%, against 35¢ hedged**. The seven bet-and-get offers carry roughly $1,350 of bonus bets. Unhedged, they are worth about **$730 expected after tax, against $480 hedged**.

The price is variance. My simulation, which treats each token as an independent bet, gives a 5% chance of ending below about $370 and a 13% chance of doing worse than hedging. Shorter odds do not help: at +200 the expected value falls to about $650, with a similar worst case. For an itemizer the hedge is cheap insurance, costing about 7¢ per dollar of bonus; for a non-itemizer it costs about 19¢. The refund offers are different, because their qualifier is a real $1,000–$1,500 cash bet. A non-itemizer loses money on them if hedged and takes a coin flip with that much cash if not, so they are not worth taking at all.

Two loose ends need a tax preparer. Kalshi legs have no IRS classification yet. Practitioners variously treat event contracts as Section 1256 futures-style contracts, as capital assets or as wagers, and sports contracts are the most likely to count as gambling ([PredictionMarketLaws](https://predictionmarketlaws.com/guides/prediction-market-taxes); [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax)). If a Kalshi loss counted as a capital loss, the phantom income on a Kalshi-hedged bonus would largely vanish: about $107 kept on the $200 example instead of $70. Filing on that reading would be an aggressive position. Tax sites also disagree on whether a bonus credit is income when received or only when it wins ([Tax Defense Network](https://www.taxdefensenetwork.com/blog/do-i-have-to-pay-taxes-on-sports-betting/); [Jackson Hewitt](https://www.jacksonhewitt.com/tax-help/tax-tips-topics/filing-your-taxes/gambling-winnings/)).

Either way the IRS expects "an accurate diary or similar record" ([IRS Topic 419](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc419)). In practice that means one row per wager, reconciled to every book's activity export. Each row records the date, venue, market, odds, cash or bonus stake, payout and result. It also carries an offer ID tying each qualifier, bonus bet and hedge together, plus Kalshi order IDs and fees.

## Dealbot does the arithmetic and the ledger, never the betting

Dealbot is a paper-trading desk with no code path that can place a live order. The owner's house rules in `ops/TEAM.md` say never to place one, and require the owner's approval for any new API key, which must be trading-only with withdrawals disabled. Its Kalshi client in `kalshi/api.py` only reads data and already implements Kalshi's taker fee, round-up included. That suits the sweep, because the bot's useful work is arithmetic and record-keeping. Every action that touches a sportsbook account has to be a person's.

| Task | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Read Kalshi order books and fees for each hedge market | Dealbot | Public data, which its read-only client already reads |
| Price the bonus side and the best book hedge | Dealbot, from a licensed odds API, or the owner, from a free odds screen | Scraping the books breaches their terms; a new API key needs the owner's approval |
| Size each hedge, compare Kalshi with the books, compute conversion and after-tax value | Dealbot | Pure arithmetic |
| Track deadlines, token expiries and Hard Rock's weekly payments; send checklist alerts | Dealbot | No account is touched |
| Keep the ledger, reconcile exports, total winnings and losses for the return | Dealbot, from the owner's entries and exported statements | Offline work |
| Find promotions, read and screenshot the in-app terms | Owner | Promotions live in the apps and never appear in odds feeds |
| Open and verify accounts, deposit, withdraw | Owner | Age 21+ and identity checks |
| Place every sportsbook bet | Owner, on their own phone, in Arizona | Automation bans and per-wager geolocation |
| Place each Kalshi hedge | Owner, in the Kalshi app, using Dealbot's contract count and limit price | Kalshi's API allows it, but Dealbot's house rule forbids live orders |
| Report each fill back to the ledger | Owner | One line per bet |

A bot that placed sportsbook bets would break DraftKings' ban on automated means ([DraftKings Arizona terms](https://sportsbook.draftkings.com/legal/az-terms-of-use)) and FanDuel's ban on robots, scrapers and "sniping" software ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/fanduel/fanduel-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-043908/prohibition-on-unauthorized-automated-access/)). It would also break the account-access terms Arizona makes every book include ([R19-4-134](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R19-4-134)). Run from a cloud server outside Arizona, it would fail the per-wager geolocation check as well ([R19-4-117](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R19-4-117)), which turns a terms problem into a regulatory one. The consequences would be voided bets, withheld winnings and closed accounts. Even bet-sync trackers that log in with your credentials look like the credential sharing DraftKings' promotion terms penalize ([DraftKings promotion terms](https://www.draftkings.com/p65terms)). Results should therefore reach the ledger by hand or from exported statements.

A bot could place the Kalshi hedge through Kalshi's API once a person confirms the book bet, and the API is built for that. It would, however, be Dealbot's first live order. The sweep involves about 50 hedges over five weeks, at two or three minutes each by hand, so changing the house rules is not worth it. What is worth building is small and paper-safe: a hedge calculator that walks Kalshi's public order book with the existing fee function, a calendar of token expiries, and a per-wager ledger with an after-tax summary.

Free tools cover the rest. Unabated's free odds screen, or Betwatch, which is free during its beta, shows the books side by side ([XCLSV](https://xclsvmedia.com/unabated-review-2026-premium-sharp-bettor-tool-worth-it/); [Betwatch](https://betwatch.io/best/odds-screens)). Paid suites pay off only for ongoing reload or positive-expected-value betting: OddsJam is reported at roughly $200–$500 a month ([The Sport Stack](https://thesportstack.io/learn/oddsjam-alternatives)), and Unabated Premium costs $199. If the owner wants Dealbot to price markets itself, The Odds API's free tier of 500 credits a month covers about 16 lookups a day, and $30 a month buys 20,000 credits ([SportsAPIs.dev](https://sportsapis.dev/apis/the-odds-api)). That would be a new key for the owner to approve.

## Conclusion

The 2026 tax rules invert the logic that made matched betting popular. Its appeal was the hedge: a locked profit whatever the result. For a filer who takes the standard deduction, the hedge is now the expensive part, because a losing hedge deducts nothing while every winning leg is taxed in full. The offers that look safest, the large refund bets, are the ones that lose money after tax, and leaving the bonus bets unhedged keeps more. The deciding variable is therefore not a sportsbook term, a conversion rate or a court ruling but a line on last year's return. The headline fight over the 90% cap matters far less here than the standard deduction does.

The project also fits Dealbot's design better than it first appears. A bot that hunts promotions and places hedges runs into three walls at once: sportsbook automation bans, per-wager geolocation and the desk's own paper-only rule. What is left for the bot is exactly where people lose money: sizing hedges at the true all-in price, tracking seven-day token clocks across eleven books, and keeping the per-wager ledger the IRS expects. Run as a five-week project with a fixed end date rather than as a new income stream, the sweep is a modest, legal windfall for an itemizer and a smaller one, with some variance, for everyone else.
