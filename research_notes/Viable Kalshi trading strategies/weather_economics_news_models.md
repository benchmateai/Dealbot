## 1. Weather: how do Kalshi weather contracts settle, what do bots and practitioners report about ensemble/model-update trading, how fast does the market absorb new model runs, and are there measured results?

### Takeaway
Kalshi's daily high/low temperature markets were settled on the NWS final Daily Climate Report (CLI) until mid-August 2026. Since then they appear to settle on The Weather Company (TWC). Rain and snow still settle on NWS. Every careful measurement found says the same thing: the market is sharper than free public model output (NBM, GFS/ECMWF ensembles, MOS, HRRR, ASOS nowcasts). An arXiv preprint finds that the market moves before the NWS forecast does. The best-documented open-source effort (the-oracle) got a pre-registered out-of-sample FAIL after fees. Taker capacity is tiny (median about $88/day). All of this evidence predates, or is mostly from before, the settlement-source switch.

### Cited Findings
**Settlement rules: before the switch (NWS CLI era; the help-center text may now be stale)**
- Kalshi's help center says daily high/low temperature markets settle on the final climate report issued by the NWS, "typically released the following morning". Determination may be delayed if the high conflicts with METAR readings or if the final report is lower than a preliminary one. NWS climate reports use local standard time. The same page says hourly temperature markets settle on TWC readings. (Read via search summary; page blocked.) — [Kalshi Help Center: Weather Markets](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823837-weather-markets)
- A practitioner guide says final CLIs cover a full day, usually 12:00 AM–11:59 PM Local Standard Time for the city, and that preliminary or mid-day CLI/DSM reports do not resolve the market. — [Wethr.net Weather Market Trading Guide](https://wethr.net/edu/trading-guide)
- Each market names its station in the rules, and tickers can mislead. A market titled "HIGHEST Temperature SATX" named station CLISDF (Louisville) in its rules. Kalshi's climate-report code maps to the NWS station ID by swapping the "CLI" prefix for "K" (CLISDF → KSDF). — [DEV Community (matveyg)](https://dev.to/matveyg/kalshis-market-titled-highest-temperature-satx-settles-in-louisville-22eh)
- Third-party station maps list LA as KLAX. One scraper says Chicago settles at Midway, not O'Hare. These are unofficial, and which listing made the Midway claim is not confirmed. — [vWeatherStation LA](https://vweatherstation.com/prediction-markets/los-angeles/); [vWeatherStation station list](https://vweatherstation.com/prediction-markets/)

**Academic evidence on market vs forecast**
- Crosier (arXiv preprint 2609.23969, not peer reviewed) extracted the hour-by-hour market-implied forecast from up to five years of Kalshi data for 7 US cities. He raced it against leading American and European public forecasts. In 6 of 7 cities the market beat the most accurate single public forecast, the National Blend of Models (NBM). The market "doesn't move with each forecast update"; the forecast "slowly publishes information that the market had already shared." The data stop at 12 Aug 2026 (NWS-settled), and the results may not carry over to TWC settlement. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23969); [FOW coverage](https://www.fow.com/insights/kalshi-prediction-markets-beat-public-weather-forecasts-study-finds); [Nextpredict coverage](https://nextpredict.io/market-news/industry/study-kalshi-weather-markets-outperform-public-forecasts/)
- A skeptical commentary says no one has shown that markets beat NOAA numerical models on 7-day temperatures. Kalshi's markets are mostly 0–1 day horizon. — [Korben](https://korben.info/en/betting-on-weather-new-business-worries-climatologists.html)

**Practitioner and bot measurements: the-oracle (most rigorous public record; notes dated May–Oct 2026, read directly)**
- The README says the bot is "not a profitable strategy" and "no strategy here has a confirmed out-of-sample edge". An S&P 500 bracket strategy was removed after a negative-edge backtest. The data come from GFS/ECMWF ensembles, NWS CLI, IEM ASOS and Open-Meteo (whose free API is non-commercial, CC BY 4.0). — [the-oracle README](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle)
- The weather-edge search dossier (2026-05-30) concludes the books are "near-efficient" and "the market has better data than our free sources":
  - Own-model bracket forecast: Brier 0.264 vs market 0.217 (significant).
  - Between-bracket reliability: realized ≈ price, CI [−0.017, +0.009].
  - NBM-gap on greater/less markets (n=776): market Brier 0.071 vs NBM 0.199, so the market is "~3× sharper than NBM". Selecting on the gap was adverse selection.
  - Longshot fade: ROI ≈ 0, "fee-eaten".
  - Monthly rain "accumulation-lock" (n=20): 0/20 lagged the market. Open-Meteo precipitation gave 3/20 false locks against the NWS gauge.
  - Corpus: 75 days, 6,768 settled markets, 17 series. Median bid-ask spread about 3¢. Books go quiet a median of about 13 h before settlement.
  - A 2026-10-01 correction says weather series charge makers no fee.
  - An earlier "+21.5% OOS" result was a look-ahead artifact from anchoring on the last traded candle instead of `expiration_ts`.
  - [Dossier](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-05-30-weather-edge-search-dossier.md)
- The intraday ASOS nowcast audit (2026-07-19):
  - Test-set Brier (n=6,209): market 0.1185, calibrated ASOS model 0.1697, climatology 0.1806, raw model 0.1834.
  - Realizable ROI by half-spread: −5.7% at 1¢ (n=494), −10.5% at 2¢, −11.8% at 3¢, −25.0% at 5¢.
  - Signals priced ≤10¢ (n=233) won 3.0%, an ROI of −53.4% (adverse selection).
  - An earlier +12.8% ROI came from picking the max-edge hour, which production cannot do.
  - Simulated lead-1 ROI was +27.2% against live realized +8.3% (n=163), about 3.3× optimism.
  - The favorite-bias map fitted on 9 cities did not transfer to new cities: gap −0.140, CI [−0.265, −0.022].
  - [New-data edge audit](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-07-19-new-data-edge-audit.md)
- The HRRR intraday dossier (2026-06-13) was a NO-GO. HRRR's mean added nothing over climatology post-peak; a sharpened HRRR σ "breaks calibration"; "HRRR is the wrong tool." The only systematic defect found was the ASOS/METAR-to-CLI settlement gap, roughly 1°F, which "appears only against CLI settlement truth." Open-Meteo's free HRRR history is a stitched series that "cannot pin a model-run init", which creates look-ahead risk. — [HRRR no-go](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-06-13-hrrr-intraday-nogo-dossier.md)
- The pre-registered OOS favorite-bias test (2026-10-01) covered 448 entries over 347 days, Dec 2022–17 Mar 2026:
  - Pooled ROI −0.5% [−8.0, +7.1] with a conservative fee, +0.1% with a realistic fee, +2.9% pre-fee.
  - 2023: −7.2%; 2024: −12.5%; 2025: −2.6%. The 2026 partial year was +17.3%. Gate Q1 was a FAIL, and the 2026 result was judged "regime or selection luck, and do not size up."
  - Maker entry at T=240 min improved results by +1.5¢ per signal [+0.5, +2.6] over a pure taker, which is "promising", but the ROI was +2.2% [−5.5, +10.3].
  - Median OOS taker capacity was $88/day (p25 $3, p75 $363); the 2025 median was $239/day. Mean spread in wide books was 5.9¢ against 3.4¢ in 2026.
  - The verdict window does not include post-switch (Aug–Sep 2026) markets.
  - [Favorite-bias OOS results](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-10-01-favorite-bias-oos-g0-results.md)

### Inferences
- **Mechanism check:** free public NWP output does not carry information the price lacks. The Kalshi price already beats NBM (Crosier; the-oracle's 0.071 vs 0.199 Brier) and leads forecast updates. So the GFS/ECMWF-ensemble → bracket-probability → Kelly design used by most GitHub bots is trading on information the market already holds. Where a model "disagrees" with the market, the disagreement mostly flags model error (adverse selection, as with the −53% ROI on ≤10¢ signals).
- **Remaining sources of edge, all narrow:**
  - (a) Settlement-source modeling. The one systematic defect the-oracle found, the ~1°F ASOS-to-CLI gap, is a settlement-mechanics effect, not a forecast-skill effect.
  - (b) Seconds-to-minutes speed on raw GRIB model runs and observations, which free Open-Meteo pipelines cannot provide.
  - (c) Passive maker quoting in zero-maker-fee weather series.
- **Regime break.** Nearly all evidence (Crosier through 12 Aug 2026; the-oracle verdicts through mid-2026) covers NWS-CLI settlement. TWC settlement, with neighbor-station/model QC and a personal-weather-station network, changes the truth variable. Pre-August backtests may not transfer. A careful trader who reverse-engineers how TWC's daily max differs from the NWS/ASOS value per station could have a temporary edge. It would likely last only months, as tools like MinuteTemp and Apify scrapers already target this.
- The TWC app integration may bring more retail flow into weather books. That would favor makers, not takers (speculative).
- **Capacity is the binding constraint even if an edge exists.** At a $88–$239/day median taker capacity, a 5% edge is about $4–12/day.
- **Win rate is the wrong metric for bracket contracts.** Buying favorites wins often and still loses after fees. The 81%-over-16-trades claim is statistically meaningless.
- **Paper-testability:** best of the three categories (daily resolution, hundreds of markets a month, history back to at least Dec 2022). The-oracle's record shows the pitfalls: about 3.3× simulator optimism, look-ahead from candle anchoring, max-edge-hour selection, stitched HRRR history, and fill and half-spread assumptions. Two of four "promising" results "evaporated under methodological scrutiny." An honest test needs pre-registration, `expiration_ts` anchoring, fill at bid/ask with a realistic queue, day-clustered CIs, and a post-August-2026-only sample.
- **DST conversion (inference):** under LST-based CLI settlement, the "day" during daylight time ran about 1:00 AM–12:59 AM local daylight time. This mattered for late-evening highs. Whether TWC uses the same day definition is unknown.

### Gaps
- Kalshi's current KXHIGH/KXLOW rulebook text could not be read (kalshi.com blocked). The exact effective date of the switch (12/14/27 Aug), TWC's day definition (LST vs local time) and rounding rules, and the TWC product used are unconfirmed.
- No source directly measures how many minutes after a 00Z/12Z GFS/ECMWF release Kalshi weather prices move. Crosier's finding that the market leads forecasts is indirect. No measurement exists for the TWC era.
- No post-switch (Aug–Nov 2026) out-of-sample performance data for any weather strategy was found.
- The cost of TWC's enterprise feed (the settlement source) is unknown. So is whether TWC's QC'd daily values are visible in real time before settlement.
- Current NOAA operational latencies (GFS/GEFS 4 cycles/day, HRRR hourly, NBM hourly on NOMADS/AWS) were not verified this session; the sourced timing notes are from 2017–2021.

## 2. Economic releases: how do Kalshi CPI, payrolls, Fed and GDP markets price relative to nowcasts and consensus, and do nowcasts beat Kalshi or Kalshi beat consensus?

### Takeaway
The evidence runs the wrong way for a nowcast trader. Kalshi prices match or beat consensus and public nowcasts. The clearest wins are headline CPI and FOMC day-before forecasts. Core CPI and unemployment are statistically tied with consensus, and GDP is roughly tied with GDPNow. Public nowcasts (Cleveland Fed, GDPNow) and consensus are already in the price. Release markets stop trading at 8:29 ET, one minute before the 8:30 print. Monthly CPI ladders are thin, and the sample is only 4–12 events a year per series.

### Cited Findings
- **Kalshi's own study ("Beyond Consensus", Dec 2025, self-published):**
  - Market-implied YoY CPI had 40.1% lower MAE than consensus, Feb 2023–mid-2025 (about 25 months).
  - MAE was about 50% lower in "shock" periods; another Kalshi summary says up to 60%.
  - Kalshi matched or beat consensus on 85% of prints measured one week out.
  - [CoinDesk, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/12/22/prediction-markets-beat-wall-street-in-forecasting-inflation-kalshi-says); [Kalshi Research: Beyond Consensus](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/crisis-alpha); [Kalshi research-arm launch](https://news.kalshi.com/p/kalshi-launches-research-arm-prediction-markets)
- **Federal Reserve staff paper:** "Kalshi and the Rise of Macro Markets" by Diercks, Katz and Wright, FEDS 2026-010 (Feb 2026), also NBER w34702 (Jan 2026). It is not peer reviewed. It covers CPI, payrolls, unemployment, GDP and FOMC from 2022 onward, against Bloomberg surveys, Blue Chip and fed funds futures. — [FEDS PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026010pap.pdf); [NBER w34702](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34702); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedgfe/102839.html)

  - In 2025, an October 2025 CPI market showed 11,748 contracts of 24 h volume. — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/35683505/markets-bet-on-whether-cpi-will-rise-more-than-0-2-in-october-2025)
- **Institutionalization signals:**
  - BMLL now sells Kalshi data for hedge-fund backtesting. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/hedge-funds-can-now-backtest-kalshi-prediction-market-prices-through-bmll/)
  - "Kalshi Courts Wall Street in Bid to Be the 'Inflation Exchange'" (23 Oct 2026, headline only). — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2026/10/23/kalshi-courts-wall-street-bid-inflation-exchange)
  - CNBC used Kalshi CPI pricing to preview the August 2026 CPI print. — [CNBC, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/crucial-cpi-report-will-show-tame-inflation-prediction-markets-show.html)

### Inferences
- "Kalshi beats consensus" is evidence against a consensus-anchored or public-nowcast strategy, not for one. If the price is already more accurate than Bloomberg consensus and at least as accurate as Cleveland Fed and GDPNow, fading Kalshi toward those numbers loses on average. The Cleveland core nowcast is a known, mechanical, free input, and its weakness (past-core-only) is visible to everyone.
- A real nowcast edge would need proprietary or faster inputs (component-level CPI models from scraped prices, used-car, airfare and gasoline data; payroll models from alt-data) that Kalshi's marginal traders lack. The Fed paper's result that core CPI and unemployment are tied with consensus says the crowd is already roughly as good as professional forecasters. Beating it means beating the professionals, with no evidence anyone does so consistently.
- **Statistical power is poor:** about 12 CPI prints, 12 payroll reports, 8 FOMC meetings and 4 advance-GDP prints a year (standard calendars). Validating a few-percentage-point edge would take years. Paper results can be checked against historical order books (BMLL sells them), but depth falls just before releases (BMLL), so the fills a backtest assumes may not exist.
- **The one measured speed gap is not available to a retail fundamental modeler.** CME futures lead Kalshi by about 0.6–1.2 s after releases; that is a market-maker/HFT cross-venue hedge. Release markets close at 8:29, so post-print speed matters only in correlated markets that stay open (FOMC, later-month and annual CPI, rate-path markets).
- **FOMC:** day-before prices are near 0/100 with a "perfect" record, so the only room is weeks out, where Kalshi and ZQ already arbitrage each other.

### Gaps
- Exact Fed-paper tables for payrolls and GDP (Table 3) and the verified headline-CPI MAE figures were not read (the PDF host was untried after the blocks; SSRN was blocked).
- No study found tests a trading strategy (P&L after fees) on Kalshi econ markets driven by the Cleveland Fed, GDPNow or a NY Fed nowcast. Accuracy comparisons are not P&L.
- Unknown: whether econ series are among the roughly 100 series charging maker fees; who the econ market makers are; and current depth near releases.
- The costs of Bloomberg consensus, a CME real-time ZQ feed and alternative price data were not sourced.
- Leads not followed: arXiv "The Shape of Macroeconomic Beliefs" (2606.30040; uses Reuters consensus; labor evidence "driven primarily by nonfarm payrolls") and arXiv 2608.30110 (LLM nowcasts of macro indicators). — [arXiv 2606.30040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30040); [arXiv 2608.30110](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30110)

## 3. News- and event-driven trading: is there evidence of profitable speed-to-news trading on Kalshi, and what does it require?

### Takeaway
No independent study shows profitable speed-to-news trading on Kalshi. Evidence is limited to anecdotes, vendor marketing and aggregate statements that bots and a small set of heavy accounts take the profits. Two measured facts set the bar. CME futures beat Kalshi by about 0.6–1.2 s after CPI, so the fastest Kalshi participants are institutional cross-venue players. On average, takers lose about 32% versus about 10% for makers. A retail LLM-parsing pipeline running at seconds of latency is behind both.

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg (28 Apr 2026): most prediction-market traders are losing money while bots rack up gains, and trading and profits are dominated by a tiny slice of highly active accounts. — [Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/most-prediction-market-traders-are-losing-money-while-bots-rack-up-gains)
- CNBC (1 Aug 2026), "Traders go full-time on prediction markets using AI bots and antennas": it profiles a 26-year-old full-time trader who reportedly collected $250,000 in one month (unverified anecdote). — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/01/traders-go-full-time-on-prediction-markets-using-ai-bots-and-antennas.html); [Blockchain.news summary](https://blockchain.news/flashnews/cnbc-traders-deploy-ai-bots-prediction-markets)
- NPR-syndicated reporting (21 Jan 2026) describes traders seeking edges through "TV antennas and Super Bowl rehearsals" — faster broadcast feeds and early event information. — [Texas Public Radio / NPR](https://www.tpr.org/2026-01-21/they-quit-their-day-jobs-to-bet-on-current-events-a-look-inside-the-prediction-market); [Capwolf](https://capwolf.com/how-traders-win-big-on-prediction-markets-using-ai-bots-and-tools/)
- A vendor describes a mention-market bot (will a speaker say word X) whose claimed edge is faster, more accurate speech transcription and LLM extraction. This is self-description only. — [Mixpeek](https://mixpeek.com/blog/kalshi-trading-bot-semantic-search-llm-extraction)
- **Latency claims:**
  - VPS vendors advertise 0–1 ms network latency to Kalshi's API but concede this is network only; fills depend on venue and conditions. A near-1 ms ping to the public REST host "is not a measure of the trading engine." — [QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/kalshi-vps); [TradoxVPS](https://tradoxvps.com/best-vps-for-kalshi-trading-bots/)
  - A capture-based guide reports a median Kalshi feed delay of about 9.4 ms for BTC book updates. Tails vary by an order of magnitude, and a bot may sometimes act on a 3-second-old book. — [Cryptostruct latency guide](https://cryptostruct.com/guides/prediction-market-bot-latency)
  - Ultra-short crypto contracts are most exposed to latency strategies; longer-dated markets leave more room for judgment. — [TradingView / Finance Magnates](https://www.tradingview.com/news/financemagnates:7f126ddf1094b:0-prediction-markets-are-turning-into-a-bot-playground/)
- CME ZQ futures led Kalshi by about 0.6 s (1.2 s elsewhere in the same article) after the 11 Sep CPI print, and depth on both venues thinned before the release. — [BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/when-kalshi-and-fed-funds-futures-disagree). A blog argues retail-heavy venues lag institutional futures after hot CPI prints or hawkish Fed speakers; no measurement is given. — [Pillar Lab](https://pillarlabai.com/blog/predicting-fed-decisions-kalshi-data/)
- Over 300,000+ Kalshi contracts, the average return was −20% before fees and −22% after. Takers lost about 32% on average and makers about 10%. Favorite-longshot bias held across categories (including Climate & Weather and Economics) but may be shrinking. — [VoxEU/CEPR column](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market); [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan (GWU WP 2026-001)](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf); [Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Mechanism:** a speed-to-news edge exists only if you act on stale resting quotes before makers pull them. The measured release-to-CME-to-Kalshi gap is about a second, and depth is pulled before scheduled releases. So scheduled macro news leaves little to pick off for anyone not co-located and hedging on CME.
- **Unscheduled headlines, mention markets and live events** are less contested by CME-hedged makers. But they are being targeted by bot builders, and the sources are anecdotes and vendors.
- **Requirements:** authenticated Kalshi API trading with WebSocket order-book and fill feeds; hosting near Kalshi's servers; a machine-readable news, transcription or broadcast feed (professional feeds such as Bloomberg/Dow Jones/Reuters are costly; costs not sourced); deterministic parsing first, since LLM parsing adds hundreds of ms to seconds; pre-staged orders and hard risk limits. Any pipeline slower than about 1 s should assume it is the one getting picked off.
- **Paper-testability is the worst of the three categories.** Paper fills ignore queue position, latency and adverse selection. A news-reaction backtest will "fill" exactly the stale quotes that faster players already took. An honest test means small live size measuring markouts (price move after your fill at +1 s, +10 s, +60 s).
- **Event-driven but slow strategies** (interpreting news over hours in thin, long-dated markets) avoid the latency race. They overlap with behavioral and favorite-longshot strategies covered elsewhere, and no measured evidence was found.
- **Legal/ethical:** "antenna/rehearsal" information edges can verge on trading on non-public information. Kalshi's and CFTC's rules on this were not reviewed here.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement was found of how fast Kalshi mention or headline markets reprice after news, or whether bots earn consistent after-fee returns there.
- Kalshi API authentication, rate limits, order types and position limits were not verified (docs.kalshi.com blocked).
- The costs of real-time news, transcription and CME data feeds are unknown.

## 4. Which categories have thin, slow markets where a careful model can beat the price, and which are already efficient?

### Takeaway
"Thin" is not the same as "beatable". Weather books are thin (3–6¢ spreads, about $88–$239/day median taker capacity, quiet about 13 h before settlement), yet their prices beat every free public forecast tested. Monthly CPI ladders are thin and still beat consensus. FOMC markets are the deepest and sharpest of the economics group. Where mispricing has been measured, it lives in settlement mechanics and in the favorite-longshot pattern, not in forecast information. The one weather regime that may be temporarily less efficient is the new TWC settlement source (since about mid-Aug 2026), which no published study has examined yet.

### Cited Findings
- **Weather is thin:** median spread about 3¢ and books quiet a median of 13 h before settlement — [the-oracle dossier](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-05-30-weather-edge-search-dossier.md). Mean spread is 5.9¢ in wide books vs 3.4¢ in 2026, with median taker capacity of $88/day ($239/day in 2025) — [favorite-bias OOS](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-10-01-favorite-bias-oos-g0-results.md)
- **Weather is sharp:**
  - Market Brier 0.1185 vs 0.1697 for a calibrated ASOS nowcast. — [new-data audit](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-07-19-new-data-edge-audit.md)
  - Market Brier 0.071 vs NBM 0.199, and 0/20 lagged rain locks. — [dossier](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-05-30-weather-edge-search-dossier.md)
  - The market beat NBM in 6 of 7 cities. — [Crosier, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23969)
  - None of three MOS strategies survived fees over 40,032 contracts. — [OddsReference](https://oddsreference.com/predictions/research/kalshi-weather-model)
- **Mispricing found was settlement-driven:** the ~1°F ASOS/METAR-to-CLI gap made low-probability bins bust about 2× more often under CLI truth than ASOS-based climatology predicted. — [HRRR no-go](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-06-13-hrrr-intraday-nogo-dossier.md)
- **Weather favorite bias does not transfer:** it failed on new cities (gap −0.140) and in the pre-registered OOS test (pooled −0.5% after fees). — [new-data audit](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-07-19-new-data-edge-audit.md); [OOS results](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-10-01-favorite-bias-oos-g0-results.md)
- **Econ CPI ladders:** thin (24 h volumes of 25–95 contracts in snapshots) — [Frenzy Capital](https://www.frenzycap.com/predictions/kalshi/KXCPI-26OCT-T0.1) — but they beat consensus on headline CPI — [FEDS 2026-010](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026010pap.pdf); [Kalshi Research](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/crisis-alpha)
- **FOMC:** perfect day-before record versus futures — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/28/kalshi-prediction-market-federal-reserve-betting-forecast-nber-working-paper/). CME leads Kalshi by about 1 s on news — [BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/when-kalshi-and-fed-funds-futures-disagree)
- **Favorite-longshot across all categories:** the pattern holds across Kalshi categories but is shrinking over time. — [Bürgi/Deng/Whelan via VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market)
- **Professionalization:** CNBC (14 Sep 2026) headline: prediction markets are "becoming more professionalized, but also harder to beat." — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/prediction-markets-efficient-win-lose-beat.html)

### Inferences
- **Efficiency ranking**, most to least efficient against public information: FOMC > headline CPI ≈ GDP ≈ core CPI/unemployment (tied with consensus) > daily temperature (efficient against public NWP, but settlement-mechanics frictions measured) > monthly rain/snow (efficient in a small sample). Ranking by "slowness" (stale quotes, thin depth): weather is slowest, but capacity is so small that a fundamental edge, if real, is worth little in dollars.
- **Most plausible pockets:**
  - (1) The TWC-settlement transition, while the crowd is still learning the new truth variable.
  - (2) Newly listed cities or stations with few traders. The evidence here is mixed: the favorite map failed, which could mean either mispricing or a model-transfer failure.
  - (3) Passive quoting in zero-maker-fee weather books.
- None has a measured after-fee edge.

### Gaps
- No liquidity time-series by category (depth, spreads, volume) for Nov 2026 was found. No measurement was found of whether newly listed weather cities are mispriced in their first weeks.
- No post-switch efficiency study (TWC era) was found.

## 5. Which strategies win after Kalshi's fees, and why? (fees, requirements, paper-testability, and confidence scores)

### Takeaway
None of the three families shows a durable, measured after-fee edge for a US retail trader using public data. The fee (up to 1.75¢ per contract at 50¢) plus a 3–6¢ spread sets a 3–5 percentage-point hurdle at mid prices. Measured model-vs-market gaps in weather are negative. Econ markets already beat the public benchmarks a nowcaster would use. Speed edges belong to faster, institutionally hedged players. The least-bad candidates are a weather maker strategy (zero maker fee) and a short-lived edge from modeling TWC settlement. Both are unproven and capacity-limited.

### Cited Findings
- **Taker fee:** ceil-to-cent of 0.07 × C × P × (1−P) per fill, peaking at 1.75¢ per contract at P = $0.50 (about $0.07–$1.75 per 100 contracts depending on price). Some sources mention per-series multipliers. — [QuantVPS fees](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained); [Predictefy](https://blog.predictefy.com/kalshi-fees); [BotForKalshi fees](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained); [PredictReport](https://predictreport.io/blog/kalshi-fees-explained); [PredictionMarketsPicks breakeven](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/ev-after-kalshi-fees-the-real-breakeven)
- **Maker fees:** most series charge makers nothing. About 100 series use a 0.0175 maker coefficient. One source says maker fees began being charged 19 Aug 2026 per Kalshi's changelog. Another describes an older flat 0.25% maker fee during major events, likely outdated. These are secondary, conflicting blogs; which blog said which is not confirmed. — [QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained); [Predictefy](https://blog.predictefy.com/kalshi-fees); [DeFi Rate fees](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/fees/); [Courtney, "Maker/Taker Math on Kalshi"](https://whirligigbear.substack.com/p/makertaker-math-on-kalshi). Weather series charge makers no fee (the-oracle, 2026-10-01). — [OOS results](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-10-01-favorite-bias-oos-g0-results.md)
- **Maker vs taker outcomes:** takers about −32% average return, makers about −10% (all categories, pre-2026 data). — [VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market)
- **Simulator vs live:** live returns were about 3.3× worse than simulated in the best-documented weather bot. — [new-data audit](https://github.com/christurgeon/the-oracle/blob/main/docs/research/2026-07-19-new-data-edge-audit.md)

### Inferences

**Fee hurdle arithmetic** (computed from the formula above plus the-oracle's 3–3.4¢ median spreads; holding to settlement, so one fee):
- At 50¢: fee 1.75¢ + half-spread about 1.5–1.7¢ ≈ 3.3¢. You need more than 3.3 pp of true probability edge over mid.
- At 10¢: fee 0.63¢, rounded up to 1¢ on a 1-lot, plus about 1.5¢ half-spread ≈ 2.1–2.5¢. That is about 21–25% of the stake, so longshot buys need enormous relative edge.
- At 90¢: about 2.1¢, roughly 2.4% of stake.
- Batching fills reduces rounding drag. A maker in a zero-maker-fee weather series avoids both the fee and the spread but takes on adverse selection.

**Bottom line for the report:** the weather category is the only one where honest, falsifiable work has been done in public. It is uniformly negative for forecast-vs-price trading under NWS settlement. The economics evidence shows Kalshi prices are, if anything, better than the public nowcasts and consensus a trader would use. If the team pursues anything here, the highest expected-value path is a small, pre-registered, post-August-2026-only test of W3 and W4 (TWC-settlement modeling plus passive quoting in zero-maker-fee weather series). Success criteria should be set in advance: day-clustered CIs, markouts, and about a 3× haircut on any simulated ROI. Capacity should be expected in the tens of dollars a day.

- The confidence scores are this researcher's judgment from the evidence above, not measured quantities.
