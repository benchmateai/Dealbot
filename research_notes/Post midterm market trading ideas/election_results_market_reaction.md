## 1. Election results: House and Senate control and seat changes, governors, Arizona, turnout, uncalled races

### Takeaway
The numbers support the "blue wave" framing. Democrats won both chambers and a majority of governorships:
- **Senate:** Democrats flipped 7 seats. The count is 54-44 with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled, and both of those seats are Republican-held.
- **House:** Democrats are at roughly 250 or more seats (NBC: 252-162 with 21 uncalled). Republicans reportedly flipped no seats.
- **Governors:** Democrats flipped five governorships: Georgia, Iowa, Texas, Ohio and Nevada.
- **Arizona:** Democrats won the top three statewide races by about 10-13 points, flipped AZ-1 and AZ-6, and voters rejected Prop 144.

All counts are unofficial. Turnout was a preliminary record of about 122.7-124 million ballots.

### Inferences
- **Senate math:** Democrats would have 56 seats if they win both Alaska and Mississippi, 55 if they win one, and 54 if they win neither. Either way they control the chamber. Both uncalled seats are Republican-held, which matches the 54 + 44 + 2 = 100 accounting.
- **House math:** At NBC's 252 called seats, Democrats are 34 above the 218 threshold. Against the 220-215 split of 2024 (my background knowledge, not verified here), 252 would mean a net gain of about 37 or more seats, if CNBC is right that Republicans flipped none. Mid-decade redistricting complicates exact seat-change accounting.
- **Turnout:** The "record" applies to raw ballots, about 124 million. The 48.8-49.4% eligible-turnout estimates may not be a record rate. My understanding is that 2018 was about 50%, but I did not verify this.
- **Arizona:** Hobbs's unofficial margin is about 12.9 points (55.51 - 42.60). Democrats won AG and SoS by about 11 points. AZ-1 and AZ-6 were both Republican-held before the election (my background knowledge for AZ-1), so they are 2 Democratic pickups. The statewide outcome reads as a Democratic sweep, not a split ticket.
- **Policy setup:** A Democratic Congress facing a Republican president with a veto implies gridlock. Legislative changes to existing law (tariff statutes, OBBBA, the defense budget) need either veto-proof margins or Trump's agreement.

## 5. Commentators' explanations and measured investor sentiment (surveys, fund flows, positioning)

### Takeaway
Commentators mostly read the result as gridlock: investigations, vetoes, and shutdown risk around a Dec 11 funding deadline. They see limited near-term power for Congress to reverse tariffs, which now rest on Section 301, or to cut the defense budget. They also read it as a de-escalation signal for the Iran war and oil, and as a source of more AI/data-center scrutiny. By Nov 6 the Fed (about 77-80% odds of a December hike) had become the main macro driver.

Measured sentiment soured. AAII bears rose to 48.4% in the week ending Nov 4, and crypto Fear & Greed sat at 25-28. Fund flows into Nov 4 were still strong: global equity funds took in $38.6 billion, their 7th straight weekly inflow.
